# Audio workspace

Updated 15 October 2026. Applies to `/dashboard/audio-editor/` and its doors. The workspace is built in eight phases (the plan is at the end). **Phases 1 to 5 are built:** converting and taking sound out of videos, many files at once, each file checked before it is offered; the waveform editor, with trimming, cutting, silence, fades, volume and ringtones, as steps that can be undone; loudness: measured, normalised to a target or a peak, or made louder, under a true-peak limiter; an equaliser and a compressor; silence trimmed at the ends and long pauses shortened; splitting a file into parts and joining files into one, with chapters; and speed, pitch, direction and channels.

## What it does (phase 1)

- **One workspace, several doors.** The Audio editor is the tool; the Audio converter (`/dashboard/audio-converter/`) and the Audio extractor (`/dashboard/audio-extractor/`) are doors into it (`partOf: "audio-editor"` in `lib/catalogue.ts`), each with its own title, help and article. The extractor starts on "Same as the file". Phase 2 adds the Audio trimmer (`/dashboard/audio-trimmer/`) and the Ringtone maker (`/dashboard/ringtone-maker/`).
- **Open** up to 50 files at once, by drop, picker, paste or the samples: MP3, M4A/AAC, Opus, Ogg Vorbis, FLAC, WAV, AIFF, and the sound of MP4, MOV, WebM and MKV videos. Files are told apart by their first bytes (`lib/audio/formats.ts`); a file that is not sound or video, or one nothing here can decode, says why and does not hold up the rest.
- **Each file** shows its kind, codec, length, sample rate, channels, average bitrate, size, tags and cover art, a waveform drawn from peaks gathered while it is decoded (200 a second, at least 2,000 and at most a million: `peakCount` in `lib/audio/view.ts`) (`lib/audio/peaks.ts`), and a player for the original and the result.
- **Formats:** MP3 (LAME, constant bitrate), M4A (AAC), Opus (in Ogg, always 48 kHz), FLAC (16 or 24 bits), WAV (16-bit, 24-bit or 32-bit float), M4R (an iPhone ringtone; at most 40 s, which the page warns of), and "Same as the file".
- **Quality** presets per codec, stereo then mono, in kbit/s (KeepSimp's own choices): MP3 96/64, 192/96, 320/160; AAC 96/64, 160/96, 256/128; Opus 48/32, 96/64, 160/96. Custom picks from each codec's list. Below 32 kHz an MP3 is MPEG-2, which holds at most 160 kbit/s; the plan says so.
- **Sample rate and channels:** the file's own or one chosen; the nearest a codec can hold is used and named. Lossy formats here are mono or stereo, so more channels are mixed down, with a note.
- **"Same as the file"** copies the sound from the file itself (never from what the page decoded) without decoding it (AAC into M4A, MP3, Opus and Vorbis into Ogg, FLAC, PCM into WAV) unless the rate, the channels, a depth or a size limit mean it must change; then it is encoded again in its own kind. Vorbis that must change becomes Opus (only Firefox encodes Vorbis), and sound that cannot be kept (AC-3, DTS, ALAC) becomes AAC; the page says which.
- **Tags and cover art** go into the new file where its format holds them, or none when the switch is off.
- **File size limit:** MP3, M4A and Opus are written at the highest bitrate whose size, less 3% and 8 KB for the container, fits (`lib/audio/target-size.ts`); lossless formats say they cannot be fitted.
- **Download** one file from the list, or all as one ZIP.
- **Offline:** the workspace, its worker and the encoders are build files, so they are in the offline copy; the samples are in `public/samples/`.

Every choice made for the person (a rate a codec cannot hold, a mix-down, a capped bitrate, a fitted size, a copy) is worked out in one place, `planFor` in `lib/audio/settings.ts`, and shown under the player as a sentence.

## The editor (phase 2)

- **Edits are steps, never changes to the file** (`lib/audio/edits.ts`). A step is keep, cut, silence, fade (in or out; even, smooth or S-curve) or volume (at most 24 dB either way), given in the times of the sound as it stood when the step was made, as the person saw it. Since steps only take sound away or change its loudness, never move it, what they do is kept against the source's own times: the parts kept, in order (`segments`), and the changes of loudness over them (`effects`, each a list of pieces with how far through the effect each runs). A later cut cannot disturb an earlier fade, and a fade made across a join is one fade.
- **Undo** is the list made again without the last change. The queue keeps each file's own list of changes (`changes`, each one or more steps undone together, as a ringtone is) and a redo list (`lib/audio/queue.ts`); a result is current for the settings and that file's steps together (`itemKey`). An edit lets the job writing that file go after a pause, as a change of settings does.
- **Joins fade.** Wherever the source was cut, the sound fades out and in over 4 ms (`JOIN_FADE`), not at the source's own ends. This takes away the click of a cut at any point, so cuts are not moved to zero crossings.
- **Writing.** An edited sound is always encoded again (`planFor(…, edited)` says so): MP3 and AAC frames are 20–26 ms, so a copy could not cut where asked. The converter trims to the first and last kept sample and, in Mediabunny's `process` hook, counts frames from the trim's start, keeps those inside a kept part and multiplies each by `gainAt` (`shaped`). Lengths, gapless headers, edit lists and Opus end granules are all for the edited length, which the check holds the result to. `lib/audio/edits.node.test.ts` holds each kind of step to FFmpeg sample by sample in WAV, and an edited MP3 to its exact length.
- **The waveform** (`components/audio/sound-editor.tsx`) is drawn from the peaks for the part in view, with cut parts closed up and fades, silence and volume at the loudness they give (`columnsOf`); the ruler's marks are at least 80 px apart (`ticksOf`). Zoom with the buttons, the keys, or Ctrl or ⌘ with the wheel (a trackpad's pinch), to 50 ms across; scroll with the slider under it or sideways on the wheel. Dashed lines mark where something was cut.
- **Choosing a part:** drag across the waveform (an edge within 8 px is moved), type the start and end (7.25, 1:07.25 or 1:02:03; `parseClock`), or use I and O at the playhead. The waveform is a slider for the playhead, with the keys listed once in `lib/audio/shortcuts.ts`, from which the help is made.
- **Listening** is a preview from the file itself: an `<audio>` element through a gain node (made on the first press, as phones require), moved past what was cut (`playFrom`) and set to `gainAt` every 10 ms. A timer, not animation frames: those stop in a tab that is not shown, and the sound would play on through the cuts. The result has a player of its own.
- **Ringtones** (`lib/audio/ringtone.ts`): an iPhone ringtone is at most 40 s and an alert tone 30, as M4R; Android takes MP3 of any length, and 30 s is used. The selection is taken, cut to that; with none, that long from the playhead, moved back if it would run past the end. It fades in over 50 ms and out over 2 s (at most a quarter of its length each), and the format is set to M4R or MP3. The page says how to put it on a phone.
- **In the doors:** the trimmer writes in the file's own format and offers keep, cut and fades; the ringtone maker starts on M4R with the ringtone panel. The converter and the extractor open the editor when asked ("Trim or edit the sound").
- Not yet: splitting into files (phase 4), loudness and normalising (phase 3).

## Loudness (phase 3, part 1)

- **The meter** (`lib/audio/loudness.ts`) is ITU-R BS.1770-5: each channel K-weighted (the shelf and high-pass, made at any rate from their analogue prototypes as libebur128 does, the printed coefficients at 48 kHz), squared, weighted (1 for the front, 1.41 for surrounds, 0 for an LFE) and summed in 100 ms segments while the file is decoded (`LoudnessMeter`). Blocks of 400 ms and windows of 3 s, one segment apart, come from those segments, so integrated loudness (gated at −70 LUFS and 10 LU under), loudness range (EBU Tech 3342: −70 LUFS and 20 LU under, the 10th to 95th percentile) and peaks are worked out for the whole file or any part, and for several parts as one (`loudnessOfParts`, an edited selection). True peak oversamples four times below 96 kHz (twice to 192 kHz) with a 24-tap Kaiser-windowed sinc per phase (β 6), flat to within 0.01 dB up to 18 kHz at 44.1 kHz. The first version had 12 Blackman-windowed taps, which droop 2.3 dB at 18 kHz: on bright, heavily limited sound (the voice preset, normalised) it read −1.05 dBTP where FFmpeg and an 8-times, 64-tap reference read −0.7, so the limiter let peaks over its ceiling. A point is worked out only where the two samples it lies between, and the loudest of the rest nearby, could make it the peak (`boundOf`), and the three phases are summed in one pass; dense noise costs about 26 s an hour of stereo on a laptop, music less, measured while the waveform is read and again in the check.
- **Against FFmpeg** (`loudness.node.test.ts`): integrated loudness within 0.05 LU of `ebur128` on a −23 dBFS tone (EBU Tech 3341 case 1), pink noise rising and falling, mono brown noise at 22.05 kHz and 5.1 pink noise; range within 0.6 LU; true peak within 0.3 dB. A tone at a quarter of the rate whose samples land on its crests is −21.57 dBTP; FFmpeg's resampler reads 0.47 dB more, so true peak is held to the known answer there instead (a tone 45° off its crests: samples −9.03 dB, crest −6.02 dBTP). The unit tests are the EBU 3341 cases 1, 3 and 5 and the 3342 case 1.
- **The limiter** (`lib/audio/limiter.ts`) holds true peaks under a ceiling: a frame's level is its sample and the points between it and the next (as the meter reads them, 6 frames later); the gain it needs is the ceiling over that level, less 0.05 dB for the error of a gain that moves. The gain applied is the least needed over the next 5 ms (a sliding minimum), released over 100 ms, then averaged over the 5 ms before, so it has fallen to what a peak needs when the peak is heard and never steps. The frames before the sound count as silence, so a loud first frame is held too. It is 5 ms and 6 frames late; the conversion lets the last frames out when the expected count is reached (Mediabunny's hook has no end call), and a decoder that gives fewer frames has the file written again to that many. It leaves sound under the ceiling bit for bit and gives the same output whatever the chunks.
- **Levels** (`lib/audio/level.ts`, the "Loudness" settings): as it is; normalise loudness to a target (−14 LUFS for Spotify, −16 for Apple Podcasts, −23 EBU R 128, −24 ATSC A/85, or −40 to −5 of one's own) with a ceiling (−0.1, −1, −2 or −3 dBTP); normalise peaks so the highest true peak reaches a level (no limiter); or louder by up to 30 dB with the limiter. A changed level means the sound is encoded again (`planFor` says so). Normalising measures each file first as it will be written (a pass of the same conversion into a `NullTarget`: edits, rate and channels as the result), so files normalised together match. The limiter makes a loud file quieter than asked, so the gain is corrected by what came out (the second time by the slope seen) and the file written again, at most twice; the try closest to the target is kept, since more gain can lift quiet parts over the relative gate and measure quieter. When the ceiling keeps a file from its target, the page says how loud it reached. A file is raised at most 30 dB, and silence or a file under 400 ms is left as it is, with a sentence.
- **What the page shows:** each file's loudness, range and true peak with its facts; the selection's in the editor (as in the file, before fades and volume, when there are any); short-term loudness drawn under the waveform, through the edits; and after writing, what was done ("Made 4.3 dB louder to −14 LUFS; the limiter took at most 1.2 dB off…") and what the check measured of the decoded result. The check holds a normalised result to its target within 1 LU and a lossless result to its ceiling within 0.2 dB; lossy encoders add a little to peaks, which −1 dBTP leaves room for.
- **Doors:** Normalize audio (`/dashboard/normalize-audio/`, "Same as the file" and −14 LUFS) and Volume booster (`/dashboard/volume-booster/`, +6 dB).
- Targets: Spotify's loudness normalisation page (−14 LUFS), Apple Podcasts' audio requirements (−16 LKFS ±1 dB, true peak not over −1 dB), EBU R 128 and ATSC A/85. YouTube publishes no figure, so it is not named.

## Tone, dynamics and silence (phase 3, parts 2 and 3)

- **Equaliser** (`lib/audio/tone.ts`): biquads from Robert Bristow-Johnson's Audio EQ Cookbook: a 12 dB-an-octave high-pass (rumble, 80 or 120 Hz), a low-pass, a bass shelf under 120 Hz, a mid bell at 1 kHz (Q 0.8) and a treble shelf over 8 kHz, each ±12 dB. Presets (KeepSimp's choices): Flat, Voice clarity, More bass, Cut rumble, Telephone (300 Hz to 3.4 kHz). Custom keeps its own state, so it stays chosen when its bands match a preset.
- **Compressor** (`lib/audio/dynamics.ts`): a soft-knee curve on the loudest channel (so the image never leans), its peak held and let fall over the release, the gain lowered over the attack and raised over the release. Presets: Gentle (2:1 above −18 dBFS), Voice (3:1 above −24, quick), Music (2.5:1 above −16, slower). It gives nothing back by itself; the page says to normalise loudness to bring the level back up, and says how much the compressor took off.
- Both run as one setting (`lib/audio/sound.ts`, "Tone and dynamics") on every pass, the measuring one too, so a file is normalised as it will sound. When they are on and the loudness is left as it is, the limiter still keeps peaks under −1 dBTP (`SAFE_CEILING`), and the check holds a lossless result to it.
- **Silence** (`lib/audio/silence.ts`): found from the waveform's peaks (5 ms) through the edits, so a silenced part is silent and a cut part is gone; under −60, −50 or −40 dBFS. "Trim silence at the ends" is one keep step leaving 0.1 s each side; "Shorten long pauses" cuts every pause inside longer than 0.5, 1 or 2 s to 0.4 s, the last first so each cut is in the times as they stand, all as one change. The panel says what it found before anything is done.
- Tests: `tone.test.ts`, `dynamics.test.ts`, `sound.test.ts`, `silence.test.ts` (100%); `level.node.test.ts` cuts a 40 Hz hum by more than 10 dB with a 1 kHz tone within 0.2 dB, and compresses then normalises to −16 LUFS with the loudness range narrowed, measured by `ebur128`; `e2e/audio-loudness.spec.ts` shapes a file with Voice clarity and the voice compressor to −16 LUFS and raises the bass with the keys; `e2e/audio-editor.spec.ts` trims a generated recording's silent ends and shortens its pause, to the sample length FFmpeg reads.

## Splitting and joining (phase 4)

- **Splitting** (`lib/audio/split.ts`, `components/audio/split-panel.tsx`): every few minutes (the last part what is left), into 2 to 500 equal parts, at markers, or at pauses longer than 0.5, 1 or 2 s under −50 dBFS (each part ending at the middle of a pause, or with the pauses left out, 0.1 s kept each side). Points closer than 0.1 s to each other or to an end are dropped. The parts are listed before anything is written. A part is the file written with one more step, a keep of its stretch (`partStep`), by the same conversion and check as any result (a `split:<id>` job in the worker, after the selected file and before the rest), with the settings it was asked with; it says so when the file or the settings changed after. Parts are named "name-01" and so on, the number as wide as the count needs, and come one by one or as one ZIP.
- **Markers** are kept per file in the source's own seconds (`marks`), so edits never move them; one in a cut part is not shown or used. The button or M on the waveform adds one at the playhead; each is listed with a × to remove it and its time to go to it.
- **Joining** (`lib/audio/join.ts`, `components/audio/join-panel.tsx`, `joinAudio`): the files that are read, in the list's order (moved with arrows), each as edited, straight on, with 0.5, 1 or 2 s of silence, or crossfaded over 0.5 to 4 s at equal power (cos and sin), a crossfade never longer than half of either file. Each file goes through the same pass as a conversion (edits, rate, channels, equaliser, compressor, its own gain), but its frames are handed to the `Joiner` instead of an encoder; the joiner holds back only the crossfade's worth of the end of each, then one limiter, one meter and one `AudioSampleSource` make the joined file. Its plan is the first file's, with the most channels any file has, always encoded again; normalising measures and brings each file to the target by itself, so they match. A null WAV output still wants one sample, so each pass gives it one. The result is checked against the length worked out from the files and the joins.
- **Chapters** (`lib/audio/chapters.ts`), one for each file joined, named by its title tag or its file name, where it starts (with a crossfade, where the fade begins): ID3v2.4 CHAP frames and a CTOC in an MP3 (into Mediabunny's tag, or a tag made for them; a tag of another version is left alone), and a Nero `chpl` list in moov/udta in an M4A (at most 255; udta made when there is none, chunk offsets moved with `shiftChunks`). FFprobe reads both, the sound decodes as it did, and the page says Apple's apps read another kind.
- Doors: Audio splitter (`/dashboard/split-audio/`, the editor open with the split panel) and Join audio (`/dashboard/join-audio/`, MP3 at first).
- Tests: `split.test.ts`, `join.test.ts` (100%); `chapters.node.test.ts` (FFmpeg-made MP3s and M4As, moov before or after the sound, read by FFprobe, decoded unchanged); `join.node.test.ts` (straight joins sample for sample, a gap of silence, a crossfade's length, an MP3 of exactly its length with two chapters, two loudnesses matched); `e2e/audio-join.spec.ts` (equal parts and a ZIP, markers with the keys, joining with chapters, reordered, crossfaded and normalised, on five profiles).

## Speed, pitch and channels (phase 5)

- **Tempo, keeping the pitch** (`lib/audio/stretch.ts`): WSOLA (Verhelst and Roelands, 1993) with 40 ms periodic Hann frames half a frame apart, each taken from within ±12 ms of where the tempo puts it, at the start whose first half best matches the natural continuation of the last frame (normalised cross-correlation of the channels summed, every fourth place on every fourth frame, then each place around the best). The channels share every choice. Before the first frame the sound counts as itself, so it starts at full level. It streams, holding a few frames; matching and adding read the held arrays directly, which made it six times faster (0.26 s a minute of stereo on a laptop).
- **Pitch, keeping the length**: stretched by tempo over the pitch's ratio, then resampled by the ratio (`lib/audio/resample.ts`: a Kaiser-windowed sinc, 16 zero crossings, β 8, from a table at 1/512 of a frame, its cutoff lowered when speeding up so nothing folds back). ±12 semitones. **Tape speed** (Keep the pitch off) only resamples.
- **Channels**: swap, left or right in both, balance (each side at most full), and karaoke: the side of a stereo mix ((L − R) / 2, and its negative on the right) plus the middle under 150 Hz, so the bass stays. The page says it is not a real vocal remover. A mono result says its channels are not changed; a mono file says karaoke would leave only the bass.
- `Changer` (`lib/audio/change.ts`) runs channels, then stretching, then resampling, after the equaliser and compressor and before the gain and limiter, in every pass (the measuring one too). It gives exactly the input's frames over the tempo (`framesAfter`), cut or made up with silence; lengths, gapless headers, Opus ends, edit lists, the check, joins and chapters all use the changed length.
- **Backwards** needs the whole sound, so it goes through the joining engine: what the joiner lets out is kept as blobs of interleaved floats (which the browser may keep on disk) and then given out from the last, each blob reversed, to the limiter, meter and encoder. A joined file is reversed as a whole and gets no chapters. Reversing a WAV gives every sample of the file, last first.
- Door: Change audio speed (`/dashboard/change-audio-speed/`, 1.25 times at first).
- Tests: `change.test.ts` (tempo at 0.5, 0.8, 1.25 and 2 keeps a 440 Hz tone and gives the exact length, pitch up an octave and down a fifth, both at once, tape, no aliasing, channels, karaoke keeping bass and removing the middle; 100% with `stretch.ts` and `resample.ts`); `change.node.test.ts` (FFmpeg reads the lengths and notes, a reverse and a swap sample for sample); `e2e/audio-speed.spec.ts` (five profiles).

## Noise reduction and repair (phase 6)

- **Background noise** (`Denoiser`, `lib/audio/repair.ts`): RNNoise (Jean-Marc Valin, Xiph.org and Mozilla; BSD-3-Clause), from `@shiguredo/rnnoise-wasm` (Apache-2.0), which is one script with its WebAssembly and model inside (4.8 MB, 3.1 MB with Brotli). It is not bundled: the worker imports it from `/rnnoise/<version>/rnnoise.js` (`lib/audio/rnnoise-files.ts`, served by `app/rnnoise/[version]/[file]/route.ts`) only when the strength is above 0, and the service worker keeps it on first use, as it keeps OCR. Each channel is resampled to 48 kHz, given to RNNoise 480 samples at a time in 16-bit scale, its delay of 960 samples taken off (measured on real speech), mixed with the sound as it was by the strength, and resampled back, so exactly the frames that went in come out, in place. RNNoise was trained on speech; the slider's hint, the help and the article all say it is made for voices and can take wanted sounds out of music. When RNNoise changes, change `RNNOISE_VERSION`.
- **Hum** (`Hum`): notches at 50 or 60 Hz and every harmonic up to 1 kHz, each 3 Hz wide (Q = f / 3), so a tone 10 Hz away is left as it was. **Rumble** is the equaliser's high-pass ("Cut rumble", phase 3).
- **Clicks** (`Declicker`): a run of at most 2 ms whose distance from the line between its neighbours is over 30 times the mean of that distance within ±5 ms (speech's own jumps reach about 20) is drawn over with a cubic Hermite curve from the samples either side, which must be outside the run. It holds back 20 ms until what follows is known, and counts what it drew over; the page says how many. Drums and consonants are longer and are left alone.
- **Harsh "s"** (`Deesser`): the sound over 5 kHz (the sound less a low-pass at 5 kHz) is turned down while its envelope (1 ms attack, 60 ms release) is over the threshold: gentle −30 dB at 3:1 down to −6 dB, strong −36 dB at 4:1 down to −12 dB. The page says how far it went.
- **Order:** clicks, hum and noise run before the equaliser and compressor, the de-esser after them; all in `SoundShaper` (`lib/audio/sound.ts`), so the measuring pass hears them too. A stage that holds frames makes the pass rewrite on a decoder that stops short (`EndedEarly`), as the limiter does.
- **Before and after:** the Original and Result switch carries on from the same moment, playing if it was, so the two can be heard one after the other at the same place. The editor's own preview plays the steps, not the sound settings.
- Nothing about the sound or its noise leaves the device; there is no noise profile to send.
- Door: Remove background noise (`/dashboard/remove-background-noise/`, all of RNNoise's result at first), with the sample `public/samples/keepsimp-voice.mp3` (eSpeak NG's voice with pink noise and a 50 Hz hum, made by `scripts/sample-audio.sh`).
- Tests: `repair.test.ts` (100%: a fake RNNoise for alignment at 48 and 44.1 kHz, notches, clicks at the ends and edges, a drum left alone, the de-esser); `repair.node.test.ts` (the real RNNoise in Node on the eSpeak NG fixture with FFmpeg's noise and hum: noise in the pauses down by more than 12 dB, speech within 3 dB, half strength between; hum at 50 and 150 Hz down by 20 dB with speech within 0.5 dB; five clicks found exactly); `e2e/audio-repair.spec.ts` (five profiles: the sample cleaned by more than 10 dB in its pauses, every sample where it was, before and after at the same moment, the hum down by 20 dB, five clicks counted and drawn over within 0.05 of the tone, axe and 320 px); `e2e/offline.spec.ts` (RNNoise kept on first use and run with the server stopped).

## Tags (phase 7, part 1)

- **The model** (`lib/audio/tags.ts`): title, artist, album, album artist, year, track and tracks in all, disc and discs in all, genre, comment, lyrics, and the front cover (JPEG or PNG, under 16 MB). A change names only the fields it sets (an empty one takes the field away) and the cover apart (a picture, `null` for none, or absent to leave them). Each file keeps its own change in the list (`tagChange`, the `retag` action); a field set back to what the file has is no change, and the change is part of the file's result key.
- **In place** (`lib/audio/retag.ts`): when the sound is copied into the same kind of file (`keepsKind`: MP3, FLAC, WAV, Ogg/Opus, M4A from an MP4 without video, with no edits), only the part of the file that holds the tags is written, and every byte of sound and every tag not named is kept. ID3v2 (`id3.ts`): frames named by the change are written anew, the rest kept byte for byte (chapters, ReplayGain, an encoder's notes); 2.4 is written as UTF-8 and 2.3 as Latin-1 or UTF-16 with its own sizes and TYER; frames that ask to be dropped when a tag changes are dropped; the tag keeps its size when at least ten bytes of padding remain (ExifTool takes fewer for a broken frame), otherwise it grows with 1 KB; ID3v1 at the end is kept in step. FLAC (`flac-tags.ts`): the comment and picture blocks are written anew, the rest kept, padding taking up the difference (4 KB more when it grows). Ogg (`ogg-tags.ts`): the comment header (with Vorbis' setup packet, which shares its pages) is laid into new pages; the pages of sound are kept, renumbered with new checksums only when the header takes more or fewer pages. MP4 (`mp4-tags.ts`): the `ilst` items named are written anew; iTunes' `free` box beside the list takes up the difference when it can, otherwise the boxes around grow and the chunk offsets move. WAV (`wav-tags.ts`): an id3 chunk holds every field, INFO the Latin-1 ones Windows reads. Vorbis comments (`vorbis-comments.ts`) replace each field under every name it goes by.
- **Refused, then written another way:** ID3v2.2 and unsynchronised tags, a second ID3 tag after the first, frames that do not add up, Ogg files with more than one stream or headers that do not end on a page, fragmented MP4, an MP4 whose chunk tables cannot all be read or whose 32-bit offsets would pass 4 GB, RF64. These go through the Mediabunny copy with the change applied to the tags it read (`withChange`), as does every file written in another format or encoded again.
- **The page** (`components/audio/tag-panel.tsx`): the fields for one file, or for all at once (a field that differs says so and stays as each file has it until typed in); what is typed is committed after 400 ms or on leaving the field, so a word typed is one write; numbers are checked as typed; covers are chosen, previewed and taken away (taking it away takes every picture). The panel says whether only the tags are written or the tags go with the sound.
- Door: Edit audio tags (`/dashboard/edit-audio-tags/`, the tune and the voice note as samples).
- Tests: `tags.test.ts`, `id3.test.ts`, `retag.test.ts` (100%: hand-made files for every refusal and edge, from extended headers and iTunes' plain sizes to Vorbis headers over many pages and a stco offset near 4 GB); `retag.node.test.ts` (files FFmpeg makes of every kind, MP3 2.4 and 2.3 with ID3v1, MP3 with no tag, ADTS, FLAC, M4A with the movie box first and last, Opus, Vorbis, WAV: every field and a cover written, read back by FFprobe, ExifTool with no warning and Mediabunny, FFmpeg's hash of the sound's packets unchanged, a second change, taking every tag away, and a 270 KB cover that spans many Ogg pages, each decoded by FFmpeg without a word); `e2e/audio-tags.spec.ts` (five profiles: the samples changed together and apart with a cover, the sound byte for byte and the file itself again when its tags are put back; M4A, Opus and WAV in place; MP3 written with the tags; problems said; axe and 320 px).

## Recording (phase 7, part 2)

- **Capture** (`public/worklets/capture.js`, plain JavaScript an audio worklet loads by its address, kept offline like any file of the site): the microphone's sound as the page hears it, 32-bit floats at the device's rate, handed over every 1,024 frames and transferred, not copied; on "flush" it hands over what it holds, so Stop keeps the end. The node's output goes to a silent gain, since some browsers run only a node whose sound goes somewhere.
- **The take** (`lib/audio/record.ts`, `Take`): interleaved floats let go of as blobs a second at a time (which a browser may keep on disk), never held whole; a mono microphone is copied to both channels of a stereo take; a pause fades the last 5 ms out and the next 5 ms in, so the join does not click; at the most a WAV can say it holds (4 GB) the take stops and says so. It becomes a WAV of floats (format 3 with its fact chunk, `floatWavHeader`) named `recording-YYYY-MM-DD-HHMMSS.wav` (no spaces), added to the list and shown, where it is edited and written like any file; the recorder's door writes MP3 at first.
- **The panel** (`components/audio/recorder.tsx`): the microphone is asked for only when Record is pressed, listed by name once allowed; mono or stereo; the browser's echo cancelling, noise suppression and automatic level are off unless "Let the browser clean up the voice" is on, so the sound is what the microphone gives; a level meter (a `meter` role, ten updates a second) that turns and says when the sound reaches full scale; Pause, Go on, Stop and keep; the screen kept awake while recording where the browser allows it; refusals said in a sentence (not allowed, no microphone, in use, no secure page).
- **Draft** (`lib/audio/record-draft.ts`): only while "Keep a draft while recording" is on (off every time), the take is kept in IndexedDB (`keepsimp-recordings`) as bytes a second at a time with what it is; the next visit offers to add it to the files (up to the last second before it stopped) or delete it; finishing a take deletes it. Listed on the privacy page.
- Doors: Voice recorder (`/dashboard/voice-recorder/`); the editor has "Record from a microphone" a press away.
- Tests: `record.test.ts` (100%: the header, levels, names, every sample kept across blocks, mono to stereo, the fades, the limit); `e2e/audio-recorder.spec.ts` (five profiles: a take with a pause, 32-bit floats as long as the clock said, the 440 Hz tone it heard measured window by window, then MP3; a draft that a reload does not lose and that is deleted once used; a refused microphone said; axe in both themes, 320 px, the editor's button). Headless browsers on a Mac cannot open even a fake device past the system's permission, so the tests give the page a microphone made by the web (an oscillator's media stream) and everything after it is the browser's own. A real microphone is to be checked by hand.

## How it is built

- **Mediabunny** (already used by the animation converter) reads and writes every container, decodes and encodes through WebCodecs, copies tracks, resamples and remixes, and reads and writes tags. Only `lib/audio/mediabunny-audio.ts` touches it, and it is handed the library, so the same code runs in the worker and in Node.
- **Encoders** come from Mediabunny's extensions, which it prefers to a browser's own once registered: LAME (`@mediabunny/mp3-encoder`) and libFLAC (`@mediabunny/flac-encoder`) always, since the main browsers have none and WebKit on Linux has its own (through GStreamer) that lose the end of the sound; for AAC the browser's own (Apple's is the best there is) when a short trial (`aacEncoding`) gives every sample back, otherwise FFmpeg's (`@mediabunny/aac-encoder`, a known 1,024 samples of priming). Each is imported when first needed and starts a worker of its own from inside the audio worker.
- **Patience:** some decoders stop giving samples without an error (WebKit on Linux). Every wait on decoding has a limit (`patiently`, 15 s): a file that cannot be probed is decoded by the page, the waveform and the check give up, and a conversion with no progress for 30 s is reported and its worker replaced.
- **The worker** (`audio.worker.ts`) reads a file (facts first, then peaks) or writes one by a plan, then decodes the result again for the check. The page runs at most two (`audioPoolSize`) through the shared `WorkerPool`.
- **Streaming:** sound is decoded and encoded chunk by chunk; a file is never held as samples, so long files take time, not memory. Peaks are gathered the same way.
- **Files the worker cannot decode:** a browser may say it decodes a codec and then fail on the first packet (Safari with FLAC), so a file's first second is decoded to find out (`decodes`). When it cannot, the page decodes it with `decodeAudioData` (which plays what `<audio>` plays) into a WAV of floats (`wavFromPlanes`), makes its peaks itself, and the worker writes from that WAV with the original's tags. AIFF, which Mediabunny does not read, is turned into the WAV it equals (`wavFromAiff`, checked byte for byte against FFmpeg for 8, 16 and 24 bits, little-endian AIFF-C and float).

### Gapless sound

Encoders put samples before the sound and pad its end. Here every file is exactly as long as its sound:

- **Reading.** AAC in MP4, Opus and Vorbis say so in their containers, which Mediabunny follows (priming comes before time 0; the track's length is the sound's). MP3 says so only in the LAME header, which Mediabunny does not read: `mp3Gapless` reads the encoder delay, the padding and the frame count, and the decoded samples kept start after the delay plus the samples the decoder adds (`mp3Kept`). The reference decoder, FFmpeg, Chrome and Firefox add 529; Safari's (Apple's) adds none, so it is measured once in each browser (`mp3DecoderDelay`: noise written by LAME, whose delay is known, decoded again). Assuming 529 cut 11 ms off the start of every MP3 written again in Safari until phase 6 found it. FFmpeg decodes the same count from every fixture.
- **Writing MP3.** Mediabunny writes a Xing frame without the LAME extension, so players would add about 50 ms of silence. `lameTagged` rewrites that frame: the frame count, the byte count, and the extension with LAME's delay (576), the padding and its CRC. The seeking table is dropped to make room; a file at one bitrate seeks without it. FFmpeg then decodes exactly the samples of the source.
- **Writing AAC.** Browser encoders hand out packets from time 0 with their priming inside, and encoders differ (Apple's puts 2,112 samples, FFmpeg's 1,024). `aacPriming` measures this browser's: 8,192 samples of noise are encoded and decoded, and where they come back is the priming. `withEditList` (`lib/audio/mp4-edit.ts`) then writes the MP4 edit list (start after the priming, play the sound's length), moving chunk offsets when the movie box comes first. Browsers and FFmpeg follow edit lists; Apple's own players have long assumed 2,112 samples and read iTunes' own tag instead, which is not written ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/quicktime-file-format/appendix_g_audio_priming_handling_encoder_delay_in_aac)).
- **Writing Opus.** An Ogg file declares how many samples to skip at the start (most encoders put 312 there) and where the sound ends, in its last page's granule position. Mediabunny's file counted the encoder's padding at the end, and WebKit on Linux's encoder puts nothing first while declaring 312, so players cut the start. `withOpusEnd` (`lib/audio/ogg-end.ts`) works from the file's own count: an encoder that puts samples first writes that many more than it was given, so the pre-skip is at most what the count allows; then the end is set to the pre-skip plus the sound's samples at 48 kHz, and each page changed gets its checksum again. FFmpeg then decodes exactly the sound. Measuring through the browser's decoder was tried first and failed on Linux WebKit, whose decoder shifts sound too.
- **Exact lengths.** Every encoded file is cut to the planned number of samples in the per-sample hook: resampling can make one more.

### Exact samples for lossless formats

Decoders hand out floats; Mediabunny's conversion to whole numbers scales by one less than full scale and truncates, which lowered every positive sample by one. For FLAC and integer WAV the hook turns floats into whole numbers itself (`asIntegers`: times full scale, rounded, clamped), from the decoder's own floats, so FLAC from FLAC is the same samples as the original.

### The check

Each result is opened again and decoded from start to end (`hearAudio`, with the same gapless rules), and `checkResult` holds its length (within about two codec frames, or 0.25 s for copied sound, which is cut on frames), sample rate and channels against the plan; an MP3 more than 20% under its bitrate (tags and cover only add bytes), and a silent result from a sound source, are reported. When the browser cannot decode the format written (FLAC in Safari), the page says it was not listened to here.

### Things found while building

- `new Quality(number)` is not a bitrate in Mediabunny: an MP3 "at 192 kbit/s" came out at 34. `new Quality({ bitrate })` is; and Opus refused the former outright. The MP3 bitrate check was added because the length check could not see this.
- **Cancelling a conversion can wait for ever** while an encoder's own worker is busy (Mediabunny's `Output.cancel` waits on a lock the conversion holds). A worker that stops a job therefore says `retired`, and `WorkerPool` ends it and starts another; ending it ends the encoder's worker too. Before this, about one run in four of the browser suite stalled.
- **The pool asks for its next job before the page draws again,** so it saw a file that had just finished as still waiting and wrote it twice. The page's dispatch now updates the pool's view of the files at once.
- **Downloads use a second, untyped link to the same bytes:** with `audio/mp4`, Firefox saves an M4R as `.mp4`, and Safari on a phone opens sound to play instead of saving it. The page plays from the typed link.
- **The player has a fixed height,** since it grows when it has read a file and moved the list under it.
- Port 4190 is ManageSieve, which Firefox and WebKit refuse to load.
