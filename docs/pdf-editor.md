# PDF editor

Changes PDFs in the browser. Nothing is uploaded: the file is opened, changed, drawn and written by MuPDF as WebAssembly in the worker the other PDF tools use. Live at `/dashboard/pdf-editor/`, and at `/dashboard/pdf-annotator/`, which is the same editor opened for marking up and commenting.

The editor is built in ten phases, each shipped before the next begins. **Phases 1 and 2 are built:** the workspace and adding things to pages; comments; and stamps, call-outs and measuring lines. The plan for the rest is at the end.

## What it does (phase 1)

- **Open** one PDF: drop, picker, paste or the sample. A locked file asks for its password; a damaged one is repaired where possible, and the file's card says so. What is not a PDF, or cannot be opened for editing, is refused with the reason. One file is edited at a time.
- **The editor's window** is the reader's window (`docs/pdf-reader.md`) with two rows added under its bar: the tools, and the look of what is selected. It has the reader's column of pages, zoom, strip of small pages, bookmarks and search. Each row is one line that scrolls sideways where it does not fit, so the pages under it never jump when the selection changes.
- **Tools:** select (V), text (T), box (B), ellipse (O), line (L), arrow (A), draw (D), cover (C), and a button that adds a picture (PNG, JPEG, WebP, AVIF, GIF, BMP; also by pasting). After a thing is made, the selection tool is in hand again and the thing is selected.
- **Every thing** can be moved, resized by eight handles (text by its two sides, lines by their ends), turned by a round handle (settling on every eighth of a circle when it comes within four degrees), duplicated, copied, cut and pasted, brought forward or sent back, and deleted. Guides show where it lines up with the page or another thing, and it snaps there.
- **Text boxes:** three families that ship with the site (Liberation Sans, Serif and Mono; SIL Open Font License), each with bold and italic, a size from 4 to 288 points, a colour, and alignment. The first line starts at the point that was clicked and has that point at half its height. Enter or a double click opens a box again; an emptied box goes away.
- **Looks:** nine colours, "none" for a shape's edge or inside (never both), four thicknesses, and how see-through a thing is. A look chosen while a tool is in hand is the look of what the tool makes next.
- **Cover** is a filled box, white unless another colour is chosen. The bar, the help and the article all say that it only covers: the words under it stay in the file. Redaction is phase 7.
- **Undo and redo** for everything, back to the file as it was opened. The buttons say what they would undo ("Undo: Move box").
- **Keyboard:** Tab reaches every thing on a page (each is a button with a name: "Text box: Dear Ada", "Arrow"); arrows nudge by one point, ten with Shift; Delete removes; Ctrl or ⌘ with C, X, V, D, A, Z, Shift+Z; `[` and `]` change the order; Enter types in a text box; Escape puts the tool down, then lets go of the selection, and only then closes the window.
- **Touch:** a finger on a thing takes hold of it; on empty paper it scrolls. With a tool other than select, the page does not scroll under the finger, so it can draw. Handles are larger where a finger is the pointer.
- **Download** writes the file, checks it and hands it to the browser. Behind the window, the tool's page has the file's card (with the page that was being read, as the file now has it) and the download card:
  - **Keep what you added editable.** Off, what was added becomes part of the page. On, it stays as separate things that the editor reads back when the file is opened again; other apps show them as stamps.
  - **Password**, with the shared lock fields (`components/pdf/lock-fields.tsx`). A file that came with a password keeps it unless a new one is set.
  - **The check** (below), the size, "Download again" and "Read the result".
- **Leaving:** the browser asks before a tab with changes that were not downloaded is closed, and "Close the file" asks in the page. A download makes the file "saved" at that point in its list of changes; a change, an undo or a redo after it makes it unsaved again.

## Comments (phase 2, first part)

Two families live on a page. **Things** (text boxes, pictures, shapes) become part of the page on download. **Comments** are the PDF's own kinds of note: other programs show them as comments, and the ones a file comes with are read the same way.

- **Marks on words:** highlight (H), underline (U), strike out (S) and squiggle (W). With the tool in hand, a drag across words marks from the word it began on to the word it ended on, through every line between, as text is selected; a press marks one word. A drag over empty paper marks nothing, and the bar says why ("No words there. On a scanned page, mark with the highlighter pen.").
- **Highlighter pen** (P): a wide see-through stroke drawn by hand, in three thicknesses, for scans and for what is not text.
- **Notes** (N): one press places the icon; the list of comments opens with the note ready to be typed. A note is dragged to move it.
- **The list of comments** is a panel beside the pages (over their lower part on a phone), opened by the button that shows how many comments there are. Page by page, each comment has its kind and colour, who wrote it and when as far as the file says, the words a mark lies on, its own words (typed in place, saved when the field is left), its replies, a field to reply, and a button to delete. Pressing a comment takes the pages to it. A field finds comments by their words, their replies, the words they mark or their author.
- **Your name** is typed once in the list and signs what is made from then on. It is kept in `localStorage` on this device only (`keepsimp.comment-author`); empty, comments are not signed.
- **Comments a file comes with** are listed with the new ones and can be read, answered, given other words and deleted. Marks on words, notes and pen strokes can be given another colour. Any other kind (a stamp, a call-out, a shape from another program) is shown and answered, and keeps the look it came with.
- **Picking:** a press on a comment picks it (a dashed frame), and the second row of the bar offers delete, "show in the list" and colours. Delete removes it, Enter opens its words in the list, Escape lets go. One thing is in hand at a time: picking a comment lets go of a selected thing, and the other way round.
- **Download** asks what becomes of comments when the file has any: **kept as comments** (the usual), **part of the page** (drawn in for good; a note keeps its icon and its words are no longer shown), or **left out** (also the ones the file came with).
- **The annotator** (`/dashboard/pdf-annotator/`, `mode="annotate"`) is a door into the editor, not a second tool. It opens with the highlighter in hand and shows the tools for marking up alone (the marks, the pen, notes, select and the list of comments); "All tools" brings the rest for as long as the file is open, and a tool taken in hand by its key shows them too. It has its own title, description, help and article.

### How comments are built

- `lib/pdf-editor/comments.ts` is the pure part: what a comment is, `selectWords` (from two points and a page's letters to a box for each line's run of words), `wordsUnder`, and `readCommentOp`, which checks every change the page asks for before it reaches the file.
- `lib/pdf-editor/mupdf-comments.ts` touches the engine: `lettersOn` (every letter of a page with its box, in the page as it is shown), `commentsOn` (the comments of a page, each with its replies; a reply to a reply is listed under the comment the talk began with) and `applyComments`.
- **A comment is known by the file's own number for it** (`c16`), which stays the same while the file is open, also across undo and redo: the working copy is never tidied, and the same steps made again on a fresh copy give the same numbers.
- **A change to comments is a step in the same list** as everything else (`inStep`), so undo and redo run through comments and things alike.
- **Marks are given their look by the engine** (`update()`), the way the format says, so other programs draw the same thing. **A reply is given an empty look,** set last: it is read in the talk it belongs to and draws nothing on the page. Dates are written by us; the engine writes none.
- **Only the kinds the editor makes are given a new look.** A change to another program's stamp or call-out sets its words and nothing else, and never calls `update()` on it.
- **Annotations are taken off a page one at a time, from a fresh list each time** (`takeAway`). Removing a note takes the window it opens in along, which changes the list the others were found in; a second removal from the old list silently does nothing.
- **Drawing comments into the page** uses the same routine as the editor's things (`flatten(session, doc, "comments")`), not the engine's `bake`, which takes every annotation of a file at once, the editor's kept-editable things included. A comment that is hidden is not drawn. Flattened and unflattened pages differ by at most two parts in 255, from rounding where a mark is blended in.
- **The check** counts comments in the result: all of them when kept, none when drawn in or left out. A page that only got a comment is known from the list of steps and is not compared with the original as if it were untouched.
- On the page, `use-comments.ts` holds the tool in hand, the comment that is picked and the list; `comment-layer.tsx` lays a button over every comment and draws the frame of the picked one and what a drag would take in; `comments-panel.tsx` is the list. Comments are not shown before the file has answered: they are whatever the file says its pages hold.

## Stamps, call-outs and measuring lines (phase 2, second part)

These three are **things**, like text boxes and shapes: they are moved, resized, restyled and undone the same way, become part of the page on download, or stay movable with "Keep what you added editable".

- **Stamp** (K): a word or two in capitals inside a rounded frame. The bar offers six usual ones (Approved, Draft, Final, Confidential, Paid, Void) and a field for words of one's own, which are taken when the field is left, as one step. A click places a stamp of a usual size; a drag makes one of any size. It has eight handles and turns. The frame's thickness and the size of the letters follow the stamp's size (`stampWeight`, `stampLayout`). The words are real text, set in Liberation Sans Bold. A stamp that is a picture is the picture tool.
- **Call-out** (Q): a text box with a white frame and an arrow to a point. The pointer goes from what is pointed at to where the words go, and the box is typed straight away; a click puts the words up and to the right. It is a `TextObject` with a `tip`, so typing, fonts and looks are the text box's. The line leaves the frame at the place nearest the point (`calloutLine`). Moving the box leaves the point where it is; the round handle at the point moves the point and leaves the box. A call-out does not turn. It takes the colour of arrows and lines, for its words, frame and arrow alike.
- **Measuring line** (R): a line with a mark across each end and its length written over its middle, turned with the line and never upside down (an upright one is read from below). Units: mm, cm, m, in, ft, pt. A scale of N measures a drawing made at one to N. The label is worked out from the ends each time the line is drawn (`measured`), so it cannot disagree with the line. It is a line for every purpose of the tools (`isLine`): two ends, no turning handle.
- **Fonts:** a stamp needs the bold sans and a measuring line the regular sans, whether or not any text box uses them. `fontsFor` says which fonts a thing is drawn with; the page loads them before a change is sent, and the download counts such pages among those whose fonts are cut and checked.
- **Handles** are now "grips" (`Grip`): one of the eight handles, or `tip`. `handleSpot` says where each is, for the tools and the screen alike.

## Signing, dates and ticks (phase 3, first part)

A signature made here is **ink on paper**: it shows that a page was signed. It is not a certificate signature, which proves who signed and that nothing changed since (phase 10), and the window, the help and the article all say so.

- **The signature window** (G, or the Sign button; `components/pdf-editor/signature-dialog.tsx`) makes a **signature** or **initials** in one of three ways, in black or blue ink:
  - **Draw** on a pad with a mouse, a pen or a finger. The strokes are fitted into the size a signature is placed at, in proportion (`fitStrokes`), and placed as a drawing.
  - **Type** a name in one of four fonts that look written by hand (Great Vibes, Sacramento, Kalam, La Belle Aurore). It is placed as a text box, so it is real text in the file, in a font cut to its letters. The four have one style each: `fontKey` gives the family alone, and a text box in one of them ignores bold and italic.
  - **Picture:** a photo or scan of a signature on light paper. `inkOnly` takes the paper out: the brightness that 60% of the picture is no brighter than is the paper, the darkest 1% is the ink, and the shades between become see-through in proportion, so the edges stay smooth and the ink keeps its colour. The result is cut to the ink (`seenBox`) and placed as a picture. A picture with no difference between paper and ink is refused, with the reason.
- **Remembered only when asked.** The switch "Remember this signature on this device" is off each time the window opens. Switched on, the signature (or the initials, separately) is kept in the browser's `localStorage` under `keepsimp.signature` and `keepsimp.initials`, offered the next time, and removed by "Forget it". It is never sent anywhere. What is read back goes through `readSignature`, which trusts it no more than a file; a picture larger than `LARGEST_REMEMBERED` is used once and not kept.
- **Today's date** is placed with one press, as a text box in the look that text has, written the way the device writes dates.
- **Tick or cross** (X): a click places a tick, a cross or a dot, and the tool stays in hand for the next box of the form. They are drawings (`checkMark`), so they need no font.
- **Where things land.** A signature, a date and a picture are placed in the middle of the part of the page that can be seen (`seenMiddle`), selected, to be dragged into place. A page is often taller than its window, and the middle of the whole page would be out of sight.
- **The window is a window of the editor's window.** It is rendered inside the reader's own tree (in the toolbar slot). Beside it, the reader's window would hide it from screen readers; the browser tests look for its controls by role for that reason.
- **Sign PDF** (`/dashboard/sign-pdf/`) is a door: the editor opened with the signature window ready and only the tools for filling in a page (select, text, tick or cross, sign, date). The rest are behind "All tools".

## Form fields (phase 3, second part)

A form's fields are **the file's own** and stay fields. Filling one in is a step in the same list of changes as everything else, the page is drawn again from the file, and the engine writes how the filled field looks, so other programs show the same and can fill it in again.

- **Reading** (`fieldsOn` in `lib/pdf-editor/mupdf-forms.ts`): every place a page has to fill in, as a `FormField`: its kind (text, check, radio, choice, list, signature, button), name, a label for people (the file's hint, or its name made readable by `labelOf`), its box as the page is shown, what it holds, its options as kept and as shown, and whether it is required, read-only, multi-line, a password, a comb, limited in length, a list that takes several, a drop-down that takes other words, or a date. Hidden places are left out. A field shown in several places is one `FormField` for each.
- **The controls** (`components/pdf-editor/field-layer.tsx`): a real control lies over each field, seen through until it is in use, so what is shown at rest is the file's own picture of the field. They are in the order `inOrder` gives (along a row, then down), which is the order Tab goes. They take presses only while nothing is being drawn, dragged or typed: with a tool in hand, a press over a field is the tool's.
- **Filling in** (`applyFields`): text and drop-downs through the engine (`setTextValue`, `setChoiceValue`), ticks and round buttons with `toggle`, a list by writing `/V` (one string or several) and dropping `/I`. `accepts` decides what a field takes: no line breaks in a line, no more letters than its limit, only what a list offers. A change that changes nothing is not a step.
- **Every place of the field is given its new look**, on whichever page: a round button that went off, a name repeated on each page. `pagesOf` finds them through the field's `/Kids` and their `/P`.
- **A list is drawn by us** (`drawList`, `listRows`): the engine writes no appearance for a list box and would leave it blank. Options one to a line, the chosen ones on a tinted bar, in the built-in Helvetica; set last with no `update()` after, turned with a turned page. Text, ticks and drop-downs are the engine's.
- **Dates:** a field whose format script is `AFDate_FormatEx("…")` or `AFDate_Format(n)` is offered as a date control, and the day is written in that format (`dateFormat`, `writeDate`, `readDate`). The script is read as text and never run. A format without a day, month and year, or with a time, is typed like any other field.
- **What is missing:** `unfilled` lists the required fields that are empty (round buttons of one name once, a repeated field once), `formLine` says so above the page, and "Go to the next one" sends the reader to the page and puts the keyboard in the field. Nothing stops a download with fields empty.
- **Typing is put into the file** when a field is left or Enter is pressed. Escape gives the field back as it was and does not close the window. Download leaves a field that is still in use first, so that what was typed last is in the file.
- **On download** (`fields` in `ExportOptions`): kept as fields, or drawn into their pages with the engine's `bake(false, true)`, which is all fields at once, as is wanted. The written file is opened again and its fields compared by name with what the working copy held (`formOf`); the check says how many read back, or that none is left.
- **What a file's form is** (`formFacts`): how many places, whether it has an XFA part (said when the file is opened: with ordinary fields too, they are what is filled in; without, the pages can only be typed on), and whether a script works fields out (`/CO`; said too, since nothing is run).
- **Fill PDF form** (`/dashboard/fill-pdf-form/`) is a door with the tools for filling in (select, text, tick or cross, sign, date). Its sample is a form (`public/samples/keepsimp-form.pdf`, made by `npm run samples:pdfs`).

## Rows of boxes (phase 3, fifth part)

A paper form often has a row of boxes for a postcode or a number, one letter to a box. A text box with `comb` set (2 to 60) is one line whose letters are spread over that many boxes of equal width, each in the middle of its own (`combLine`, `combStarts` in `text-layout.ts`). The number is a part of a text box's look ("Boxes" in the bar, 0 for ordinary text), remembered for the next box like the font; the box's two side handles fit it to the row on the page. A call-out takes none. While it is typed, the browser sets the letters a box apart by a figure's width, which is close; the page shows them exactly once the file has them, since it is drawn from the file. `comb.test.ts` (100% with the other unit tests), `comb.node.test.ts` (each letter's place read back with pdf.js) and a journey in `e2e/pdf-form-filler.spec.ts`.

## Signed files (phase 3, third part)

A digital signature covers the bytes of the file as it was signed. A file that is written anew has other bytes, and the signature no longer holds. Written **after** the file, the signed part is still there byte for byte.

- **`exportAfter`** (`lib/pdf-editor/mupdf-signed.ts`): the steps in force are made again on a fresh copy of the file as it came (`madeAgain`), and that copy is saved with the engine's `incremental` option, once, and let go. The engine can write a file this way only once for each time it opens one (a second time, after an undo, it wrote a damaged file), so the working copy is never written so.
- **Nothing else is done to the file:** fields stay fields, comments stay comments, added things stay the stamps they are, fonts are not cut, nothing is tidied, no lock is set. The page hides those choices while "Keep it valid" is chosen.
- **The check** reads the written file: it must begin with the file as it came, byte for byte (`signature.intact`), and its fields must hold what the working copy holds. Before anything is written, the fresh copy must hold what the working copy holds, page for page.
- **What a program that checks signatures will say:** that the signature is valid and that the file was added to after it. Filling in fields and commenting are the changes such programs expect; stamps are annotations too, but a file whose author allowed no changes at all will be reported as changed. The page says so.
- **When it cannot be done** (`canWriteAfter`): a file that had to be repaired cannot be added to. The page then says that a changed copy no longer counts as signed, as before.
- **Tests:** `tests/pdf-signing.ts` makes a truly signed file (pdf-lib writes it, `@signpdf` signs it with a certificate made on the spot) and `readSignature` works out with node-forge, not with the engine, whether the signature still holds and whether it covers the whole file. `signed.node.test.ts` and `e2e/pdf-signed.spec.ts` (five profiles) check both ways of downloading, again and again with undoing in between. Poppler's `pdfsig` agrees when run by hand.

## Drafts (phase 3, fourth part)

Nothing is kept unless a switch is turned on: "Keep a draft on this device", off by default, on the tool's page and beside the open file. On, unfinished work survives a closed tab.

- **A draft is the file as it came and the list of steps** (`EditSession.steps`), with the pictures that were placed and any font of one's own the steps are written in. That is all the editor needs: taking a draft up opens the file and makes the steps in force again (`takeUp`, which is `madeAgain` on a fresh session). The undo list comes with it, the undone steps too.
- **Where it is kept** (`lib/pdf-editor/draft-store.ts`): IndexedDB, database `keepsimp-drafts`, in this browser only. The file and each picture are kept as bytes, not as files: WebKit keeps no files there in a private window. The steps are written again a moment after every change (`use-draft.ts`); the file is written once, when the first change is made, so a file that was only opened replaces no draft. One keeping follows another, since two at once could clear what the other had just kept.
- **One draft at a time,** of the file that was last changed with the switch on. Files over 50 MB are not kept (`DRAFT_LIMIT`), and the page says so. When the browser will not keep it (no room, or a window that keeps nothing), the page says that too.
- **What is read back is trusted no more than a file** (`readDraft` in `lib/pdf-editor/draft.ts`): every step goes through the checks a change from the page goes through (`readOp`, `readCommentOp`, `readFieldOp`), in the page and again in the worker. What is not a draft is not offered.
- **The page:** a card "Unfinished on this device" with the file's name, how many changes are in force and when it was kept, and two buttons: Take it up, Throw it away. The line beside the open file says "Keeping a draft…" until the file as it now stands is really kept; only then does the browser stop asking before the page is left.
- **When it goes:** closing the file for good, throwing it away, turning the switch off, or beginning a draft of another file. A download does not remove it.
- **A locked file** is kept locked and asks for its password again when it is taken up. No password is kept.
- **The privacy page** (`app/(marketing)/privacy/page.tsx`) lists everything the editor can keep on the device: the name comments are signed with, a remembered signature, and a draft (with its pictures and fonts).

## Changing the text that is there (phase 4, first part)

"Change text" (E) offers every line of a page's own text. Click one, type, press Enter: the old letters are **taken out of the page's content**, and the new ones are written in their place **with the very font object the line was written with**. Nothing is covered, no font is copied, and none is added for a letter the file's font has.

- **Reading** (`linesOn` in `lib/pdf-editor/mupdf-text.ts`): the page's content alone is run (`runPageContents`, so that nothing the editor or anyone laid over the page is taken for its text) and read as lines. Each letter comes with its font, size, colour and place, and a line is its text, its box, where it starts, the looks its letters have (`LineStyle`: font, size, colour, how far it is raised) and which look each letter has. Lines come from the top of the page down.
- **What a font can write** (`codesOf`): the engine tells which shape a letter was drawn with, but not which number of the font stands for it, and that number is what has to be written. So a second copy of the file is opened (`EditSession.reading`), which is read and never changed. In it, a page is added that shows every number a letter can have in that font (256, or 65,536 for a font with two bytes to a letter) once, each at a place that says which number it is, and the engine is asked what it sees. That takes 5 to 80 ms a font and is kept. A shape that stands for several letters at once (the "fi" of a paper) is not taken for its first letter: written for an "f", it would add an "i" nobody typed, which is what the first try on a real paper did. Places of private use are not letters. A font that writes its letters with one or two bytes as it pleases is not written with.
- **Which look a new letter takes** (`spreadLooks`): the start and the end the old and new words share keep their looks; what was typed between takes the look of the letter before it, as in any editor. A mark that the look it was given cannot write, and another look of the line can (a raised star set in a symbol font), takes that look whole.
- **Stand-ins:** a letter the file's font lacks is written in the closest of the editor's fonts (`standInFor`: serif, sans or mono, bold and italic, by the font's own flags and its name), which is put into the file once (`addFont`) and cut to its letters on download. The field names such letters while they are typed (`standIns`), and the bar says so after. When no font has a letter, nothing is changed and the page says which letter.
- **Taking out** is the engine's redaction with nothing drawn in its place, no picture and no line art touched, and marks for redaction that the file came with set aside and put back. It is done through a thin band through the middle of each letter (`bandOf`): the engine takes out every letter a band touches, and the boxes of letters reach into the lines above and below where lines are set close; their middles do not. Links that lay over the line are put back if the engine drops them.
- **Writing** (`runsOf`, `contentFor` in `retext.ts`): letters that follow each other in one font, size, colour and height are one run; a space the font has no letter for is only room. What is written asks for everything it needs (colour, plain filled letters, no extra spacing) and is placed through the inverse of the page's own matrix, so it stands upright on a page the file shows turned. The page's content is closed off first (`q … Q`) so that what is written starts from nothing. The font is named in the page's resources as `KS<number>` (the file's own, by its object number) or `KS<key>` (ours).
- **The proof, at every change:** the page is read again. Every other line must be where it was, saying what it said, or the change is not made ("cannot be changed without touching the text beside it"); and the new words must be read back at the line's height, or it is not made either. A change that fails is taken back whole, like any step.
- **What cannot be retyped:** text that is written downwards, a letter under a letter, and text in scripts whose letters join and change shape (Hebrew, Arabic, the scripts of India and South-East Asia). Both can be taken out. The field says why.
- **Turned text** (second part) is read with the way it runs (`PageLine.along`), typed straight in the field and written turned: `runsOf` lays the letters along the line and `contentFor` turns each run's matrix. A raised letter is "to the left of the way the line runs".
- **Another look for a line** (second part; `LineLook` on the change): a size scales every look of the line by as much as the main one (`mainStyle`, `restyled`), so a raised mark stays as small and as high beside its letters; a colour is for all letters; a font is one of the editor's, and the whole line is then set in it, with nothing standing in for single letters. Size and colour keep the file's own font. The bar shows the three while a line is typed, with Done; a press in the bar does not take the keyboard from the line (WebKit gives a pressed button no keyboard, and the field took that for being left).
- **Moving a line** (second part): dragging a line's frame, or the arrow keys on it (a point, ten with Shift), writes it again as far from where it was (`move` on the change). It is one step, "Move text".
- **The stand-in is chosen by name first** (`familyOf`): what a file says of its fonts' kind is often wrong (a Frutiger marked as serif), and a well-known name is not.
- **A step like any other:** a change is found again by where the line is and what it says (`isLine`), so it can be made again on a fresh copy for undo, for a signed file and for a draft (`readTextOp` is one of the draft's readers).
- **On download** the file's own fonts are left whole unless one of ours was used on a page (then fonts are cut as for text boxes, and kept only if every page draws the same). The check says in how many places the page's own text was changed and how many lines were read back from the result.
- **On real files** (a LaTeX paper, a publisher's report, two IRS documents, a booklet; `scripts/pdf-benchmark-files.mjs` fetches them): 56 lines of 56 were changed, each read back, every page that was not touched identical to the original, and qpdf and Poppler had nothing to say about any result.
- **Tests:** `retext.test.ts` (100%), `retext.node.test.ts` with the real engine and a fixture of every kind of text (`tests/pdf-text.ts`: a font embedded with only its letters, fonts that are not embedded, two looks on a line, colour, a raised mark, a link, lines set close, turned text, a turned page), results read by pdf.js and pdf-lib, the page compared pixel for pixel outside the line; and `e2e/pdf-retext.spec.ts` on five profiles.
- **Reading can change a file.** A tagged file whose structure tree has a part that names no parent is mended by the engine while a page's content is run, and the engine lets nothing be changed outside an operation: it gave up half way and left a file that could not be written (a download failed, or the engine stopped). So `read` runs the page inside an operation of its own when no step is being made (`EditSession.working`). Found on a long report of the United Nations; `tagged.node.test.ts` has a small file with the same fault (`tests/pdf-tagged.ts`), written with its objects packed together as that report's are, which is what it takes.
- **Paragraphs** are phase 5 (below).

### A font of one's own (phase 4, third part)

A text box, a call-out and a changed line can be set in a font the person has: a TrueType or OpenType file ("Your own font"), or one of the fonts on the device where the browser can list them ("From this device": the Local Font Access API, which Chromium on a computer has and asks before using). The file is read on the device and goes nowhere.

- **What a font file says of itself** (`readFont` in `lib/pdf-editor/own-font.ts`, pure): what it is called (its full name; the name printers know it by is the name it is written under in the PDF), and whether its maker allows it in documents. A font marked restricted, or bitmap-only (`fsType` in its OS/2 table), is **not embedded**, and the bar says whose font it is. A file over 30 MB, a web font (WOFF) and anything that is not a font are refused with the reason.
- **Collections:** the fonts that come with a computer are often several in one file (a family's regular, bold and italic). The one that was chosen is found by name and **taken out as a file of its own** (its tables copied behind a list of their own), since neither the browser's `FontFace` nor a PDF takes a collection. From a file that is a collection, the first font is taken.
- **Its key** (`ownKey`): `own-` and twelve hexadecimal figures made from the font's bytes. The same file is the same font wherever it is added, which is what undo, a draft and a second adding need. It is a font family like the editor's own (`FontFamily = BuiltIn | OwnKey`), with no bold or italic of the editor's making: it is one file, and the bar offers neither for it.
- **In the file** it is embedded as the editor's own fonts are (`addFont`, then cut to the letters used on download, kept only if every page draws the same). TrueType, OpenType with PostScript outlines, and fonts out of collections were each written and read back with Poppler and pdf.js. On a page it goes by `KS` and its key with the figures kept (`oursAs`), so two fonts of one's own never share a name; `isOurs` tells such a name from a font of the file.
- **A line set in a font of the editor's or of one's own** is written with that font's own spaces, so its words stand as far apart as the font means and are read as words; a file's font that has no space still gets a gap that is only room.
- **A letter the font lacks:** in a text box it is left out and the bar names it, as with the editor's fonts; in a changed line nothing is changed and the page says which letter.
- **Drafts** keep a font of one's own that the steps are written in (`font/<key>` beside the file, as bytes), and nothing of fonts that were added and not used. Read back, it must still be a font, allowed, and the very font the steps name (its key is made again from its bytes); a draft whose font is missing or is another is not offered.
- **Tests:** `own-font.test.ts` (100%: names, permissions, collections, files that end too soon), `own-font.node.test.ts` with the real engine (a text box and a line in such a font, a font taken out of a collection, two on one page, undo, a draft, a letter it lacks) and `e2e/pdf-own-font.spec.ts` on five profiles (a file added and used for a box and a line; what is refused; the list of the device's fonts, stood in for by two real fonts in one file since only Chromium has the list and every machine has other fonts; a draft that brings its font; nothing sent anywhere). The real list was tried by hand in Chromium on macOS with fonts out of the system's collections.

## Paragraphs (phase 5, first part)

With "Change text" on, lines that belong together are one place to click: a paragraph. Its words are typed as one text and **flow again within the width the paragraph had**, with its alignment, the distance between its lines and how far its first line is set in. "Paragraphs" in the bar (on to begin with) turns that off, and every line is a line again.

- **Which lines are a paragraph** (`blocksOf` in `lib/pdf-editor/paragraphs.ts`, pure). A file does not say; it only places letters. So running text is told by what it looks like: lines in one font and size, one under the other at one distance, in one column, **each of them full**, which is to say that the first word of the next would not have fitted behind it (`PageLine.first`). A short line ends a paragraph; a line that is set in begins one. What is not taken: a stack of short lines (an address), narrow stacks (under 12 letter heights; two lines must be 18 wide, since the first of any two is "full" when it is the longer), and three lines or more that each begin with a capital, a figure or a mark (a list, a poem, the rows of a table). Columns side by side stay apart. Detection can be wrong either way, which is what the switch is for.
- **How it is set** (`shapeOf`): its edges, its first line's indent (which may hang out to the left), the distance from line to line, and left, right, centre or both edges, from which edges its lines share.
- **One text** (`joined`): lines are joined by a space. A word the file broke at the end of a line ("bor-" / "rower") is joined with a **soft hyphen** (`SOFT`), which is nothing in the middle of a line and a hyphen where a line ends at it, so a paragraph that is not changed breaks where it broke. Whether a hyphen at a line's end was the word's own cannot be known. It is kept when the word has other hyphens (state-of-the-art, English-to-German) or the page writes the word with it somewhere on a line (`wordsOf`); otherwise it is taken for a break. That is wrong for a compound that only ever stands at a line's end, so **the field names the words it joined** and asks for the hyphen where one belongs.
- **The change** (`FlowOp`, `do: "reflow"`; `reflow` in `mupdf-text.ts`). The paragraph is found again by its lines. **Only from the first line the change touches** (`firstChanged`) are lines taken out and written: the lines before it stay as the file has them, letter spacing and all. Another look, another alignment or a move writes all of them.
- **Layout** (`flow`, pure): greedy, as a word processor's. A line is broken at a space, at a soft hyphen (which then shows), behind a hyphen in a word, and where the text has a new line (Enter in the field). A word wider than the line runs over. Lines set to both edges get their spare room shared among their gaps, except the last and except where a gap would become wider than 0.7 of the letters' height: the engine reads wider gaps as the end of a line, and so would the next change.
- **How far apart words stand is measured, not assumed.** Many fonts in files have no space at all and words are simply placed; where there is one, files set letters and words a hair closer or wider than the font says. What is left of each old line's width when its letters are taken off, shared among its gaps, is the gap (the median of the lines; for text set to both edges the least, and no more than the font's own). A space the font has is written as a space where it is that wide to within a hair, else the gap is room. And the paragraph is no narrower than any of its old lines is with that gap: **a line that fitted before fits again.**
- **Room.** A paragraph that grows runs down the page, and nothing below it moves. It may not be written over other text (the page could no longer be read line by line): then nothing is changed and the bar says how many lines it needs and how many have room.
- **A page's links stay in their order.** Taking letters out takes the links that lay over them off the page, and they are put back; they are put back where they were among the page's notes, since that order is the order the Tab key visits links in.
- **Links follow their words** (`followLinks`, for lines too). A link that lay over letters that are written anew is put over those letters where they now stand, as far around them as it was (on the line most of them are on, when they end up on two). A link whose letters were typed over stays where it was; one that also lies over lines that were not touched is left alone. The place is written into the link ourselves, in the page's own measure: the engine's `setBounds` writes the place as the page is shown, which is upside down in the file.
- **On the page** (`retext-layer.tsx`): a paragraph's frame is a button like a line's (drag or arrow keys to move, Delete to take out). Its field is a `<textarea>` as wide as the paragraph, in a font close to its own, with its line distance, alignment and indent, so the words wrap about as they will; the page shows the real thing once the file has laid them out. Enter is a new line; Ctrl+Enter, ⌘+Enter, Done or a press elsewhere puts the words into the file; Escape leaves them. The bar adds four alignment buttons to a line's size, colour and font.
- **For the download's check** the engine says what each change to a paragraph wrote (`TextChange.wrote`: from which line on, and the lines that are there now), as it says the fonts; a draft keeps it (`readWrote`).
- **What was written anew stands last in the page's content.** A program that reads a page in the order it was written (pdf.js does; Poppler reads by place) reads those lines last. That was so for single lines since phase 4.
- **On real files:** every paragraph on eleven pages of four documents (a paper, a report, a tax guide, a booklet) had a word put into it: 57 of 64 were changed and every one of their 266 lines was read back; six were refused for want of room below and one because its lines could not be taken out without a neighbour's letters, each time with nothing changed. On a paper with boxed citation links, every box was on its number afterwards.
- **Tests:** `paragraphs.test.ts` (100%), `paragraphs.node.test.ts` with the real engine and a fixture of running text (`makeParagraphPdf`: ragged with bold words and a link, set to both edges in a font cut to its letters with a word broken at a line end, an address, centred lines with a line close under them, two columns), results read by pdf.js and pdf-lib; `e2e/pdf-paragraphs.spec.ts` on five profiles.
- **Not yet:** a paragraph that pushes down what is below it, paragraphs across columns or pages, and paragraphs whose lines are turned (those are changed line by line).

## Pictures the page already has (phase 5, second part)

"Pictures of the page" (I) puts a frame on every picture a page draws. A picture is dragged to move it, its corner is dragged to change its size, and the bar turns it by quarters, cuts it to a part, shows it whole again, puts another picture in its place, or takes it out. The arrow keys move it, + and − change its size, Delete takes it out.

- **How a page draws a picture, and how that is changed** (`lib/pdf-editor/page-pictures.ts`, pure). A page's content draws a picture with one instruction: the picture's name and `Do`, after the page's measure has been set so that the picture's unit square lands where the picture is to be. To change the picture, **that one instruction is written again** (`drawing`): with another measure in front of it (`q … cm /Name Do Q`), with a clip, with another name, or not at all. Nothing else of the content is touched, and the picture's data is never decoded or saved again: a JPEG stays the JPEG it was.
- **Finding the instructions** (`picturesIn`): the content is read as bytes, a letter for each, by a small reader that knows strings, comments, lists, dictionaries and pictures written into the content itself (`BI … ID … EI`, whose bytes it passes over), and follows `q`, `Q` and `cm` to know the measure at each `Do`. Only names that are pictures in the page's resources count. **Not offered:** pictures inside something else the page draws (a form), and pictures written into the content itself. On nine real files the engine draws 45 pictures on the pages tried, and 43 are offered.
- **Moving, resizing, turning** are one thing: a measure everything goes through after the picture is where it was, as the page is shown (`moved`, `scaledAbout`, `turnedAbout`), and what is written before the picture follows from it (`leadFor`), whichever way the page itself is turned.
- **Cutting to a part hides; it does not remove.** A clip is written before the picture (`l b w h re W n`), so only that part shows; **the whole picture is still in the file**, and anyone can show it again. The bar says so while a picture is cut, and the check after a download says so. (Removing what is cut away is redaction, phase 7.) A part of a part is one clip, not two; "Show it whole" takes the clip away. A picture that is turned or mirrored is not cut here.
- **Another picture in its place** is set in whole and undistorted, as large as fits where the old one showed, in its middle (`fitInto`). It is handed to the worker like a picture that is added, and a draft keeps it.
- **Taken out, a picture is out of the file** once nothing draws it: the page gets a list of its own of the things it draws by name (pages often share one), without that picture, and the download leaves out what nothing names.
- **The proof** (`mupdf-pictures.ts`): after the change the page is read again. Every other picture must be where it was, the picture must be where it was meant to go, and the page's text must say what it said, line for line; otherwise nothing is changed.
- **On the page** (`picture-layer.tsx`, `use-inpage.ts`): a picture's frame stays in place while the page is read again after a change, so the keyboard and what is chosen stay with it (the window's own keeper of the keyboard takes it away from anything that is removed); nothing is done to a picture until where it is has been read again. Changing text and changing pictures are one at a time.
- **Tests:** `page-pictures.test.ts` (100%), `page-pictures.node.test.ts` with the real engine, where each picture is painted read back with pdf.js (`paintedPictures`) and how many the file holds with pdf-lib; `e2e/pdf-page-pictures.spec.ts` on five profiles (dragging, the corner, the keyboard alone, cutting, exchanging, taking out, one tool at a time, accessibility).

## Links (phase 5, third part)

"Links" (J) puts a frame on every link of a page. A link is chosen to see where it leads and to lead it somewhere else; it is dragged to move it, dragged by its corner to change its size, and taken away with Delete or from the bar. A new one is made by dragging a box on the page, or with "New link" in the bar, which puts one in the middle of what is seen; it is in the file once it leads somewhere.

- **Where a link leads** is typed as one thing (`targetFrom` in `lib/pdf-editor/links.ts`, pure): a page ("3", "page 3") or an address. A name without its kind is taken for the web (`example.org` becomes `https://example.org/`), what looks like a letter's address for a letter (`mailto:`).
- **Only addresses of four kinds are written:** `http`, `https`, `mailto` and `tel` (`addressFrom`). Nothing that runs anything (`javascript:`), nothing that reads the device (`file:`). The same check is made again in the worker, on what arrives from the page or from a draft.
- **Where a link leads is not checked.** Nothing is asked of an address, so nothing is sent anywhere; the bar and the check after a download say that it is not checked.
- **In the file** (`mupdf-links.ts`) a link is a note of the kind Link. A new one has no border and is printed with the page. Its box and where it leads are written into the note ourselves, in the page's own measure, also on a page the file shows turned (the engine's own `setBounds` writes the place as the page is shown, which is upside down in the file). A link to a page shows that page whole (`/Fit`). Where a link that is already there leads is asked of the engine, which knows every way a file can say it (named places among them); a link it cannot follow can still be moved and taken away.
- **The proof:** after a change the page's links are read again; there must be as many as there should be, and the link must lie and lead where it was meant to.
- **On the page** (`link-layer.tsx`, `use-links.ts`): frames stay in place while the page is read again, as pictures' do. Links, the page's pictures and the page's words are changed one at a time.
- **Tests:** `links.test.ts` (100%), `links.node.test.ts` with the real engine, read back with pdf.js (where each link lies, where it leads), and `e2e/pdf-links.spec.ts` on five profiles.

## Find and replace (phase 5, fourth part)

"Find and replace" (F) looks for words through the whole file and puts others in their place, one place at a time or all at once.

- **Where words are looked for** (`findText` in `mupdf-text.ts`): on every page, in each paragraph as one text and in each line that is no part of one. So words that a line break parts are found ("day on" / "which"), and so is a word the file broke with a hyphen: a soft hyphen is no letter (`findIn` in `lib/pdf-editor/find.ts`, pure). Any run of spaces and line ends is a space. Capitals can be told apart, and whole words asked for. Lines whose words cannot be changed are passed over. At most 5,000 places are listed.
- **Every place is shown before it is changed:** the page it is on is shown with a frame around the line or paragraph, and the bar shows the words around it with what is there struck through and what will be (`preview`).
- **A place is changed as it would be by hand** (`opFor`): a line as a line, a paragraph as a paragraph that flows again, with everything that goes with that: the file's own font, stand-ins for letters it lacks (the fonts that may be needed are made ready first), links that go with their words, and the reading-back.
- **Replace all is one step,** undone by one undo. What cannot be changed is **left out and said** (`replaceText`): a paragraph that would grow over what is below it, a line that cannot be taken out without its neighbour. The step is tried; a change that fails is taken out of it, the file being as it was before the step, and the rest is tried again.
- **After every replacement the file is looked through again,** so what is shown is what the file now holds.
- **Tests:** `find.test.ts` (100%), `find.node.test.ts` with the real engine (lines and paragraphs, across line breaks, one place and all, what is left out and why, letters the font lacks, undo), `e2e/pdf-find.spec.ts` on five profiles.

## Pages (phase 6, first part)

"Pages" in the bar (M) lists the file's pages beside them as small pictures. Pages are chosen there (a press; with Shift, all from the one pressed before; with Ctrl or ⌘, one more or one fewer) and then moved, turned, copied or taken out; an empty page is added after the last chosen one, as large as the page before it. Every change is one step of the file and is undone like any other.

- **A change is worked out as a plan first** (`planOf` in `lib/pdf-editor/pages.ts`, pure): the list of pages afterwards, each with where it comes from: a page of before (turned or not), a copy of one, or an empty one. The engine makes the file fit the plan (`applyPages` in `mupdf-pages.ts`), the screen keeps the chosen pages chosen where they are afterwards (`chosenAfter`), and a download uses the same plans to know which page of the result is which page of the file as it came.
- **The engine's own way of ordering pages is not used.** `rearrangePages` also throws away the file's tags, attached files, page numbers and the list of its form's fields: a probe on a tagged file with attachments lost all four. Here a page that moves is the same object in another place (`deletePage`, then `insertPage`), with what it left to the tree of pages above it written on it first (`standAlone`), so that it is the same page wherever it lands. Links, bookmarks, fields and tags that belong to it need no care at all: they name the page, not its place.
- **A page that is taken out is gone from the file** (`forget`). Links on other pages that led to it are taken away, also those that name a place by a name; bookmarks that led to it are taken out of the list, unless others hang beneath one, which then stays as a heading that leads nowhere; what the file opens at is cleared when it was that page; the fields that were on it are taken off the form, and a field left without any place goes too; its pieces are taken out of the parts a tagged file says it is built of, through the page's own entry in the tree of parents and not by walking the whole tree, and a part left empty is taken out of the part above it, so that what a figure was said to show goes with the figure. Last, the page object is emptied: whatever still names it (a place with a name, a thread of an article) then names nothing, and cannot bring the page's content along into the result. A test looks through every stream of the result for the page's words, its note and its field, and fails when the page is not emptied.
- **A copy has content of its own** (`copyOfPage`): its content streams are copied byte for byte, and it has its own lists of fonts and pictures (the fonts and pictures themselves are shared and stored once). Without that, changing the words of a copy changed the page it was a copy of. Notes and links are copied, each with a look of its own, and an answer to a note answers the note's copy; a form's fields are not, since a field has one place, nor the page's place in the file's tags.
- **Turning a page** writes its turn on it. What the editor added to it is written again so that it stays where it is on what the page shows (`onTurnedPage` in `objects.ts`): in the file's own points it has not moved at all, which a test reads with pdf.js. Comments, fields and links are in the file's own points and turn with the page by themselves.
- **The numbers a file gives its own pages** (i, ii, iii, then 1, 2, 3) are kept by us (`runsOf`, `writeRuns`, and `runsAfter`, pure). The engine keeps them itself when a page is put in or taken out, and gets it wrong at the front of a file: a page put there was numbered 1 and the count began anew after it, and a file that numbered nothing was given numbers. A run grows and shrinks with its pages; a page that is put elsewhere, copied or added joins the run where it lands; each run counts on from its start, so there are no gaps.
- **The screen follows the list.** News from the worker carries every page's size and the bookmarks as they lead now whenever the list changed, also after an undo (`laidOut` on the session, `layout` in `EditNews`); the pages named in it are the places that hold another page than before. Each tool lets go of what it had chosen (a line, a picture, a link, a comment, what was found), which may be on another page or on none. A page's picture is counted up and never back, so that a place that is given up and taken again never shows a picture of what was there before.
- **The download says what happened** (`pagesChanged`, `report.ts`): in how many steps the list was changed; that pages taken out are no longer in the file, with how many links, bookmarks and fields went with them; and, written after a signed file, that they are still in the signed part, where a program can find them. Pages that only moved are compared with the page of the original that each is. Lines of the page's own text that were changed are read back from the page they are on now.
- **The list** (`components/pdf-editor/pages-panel.tsx`, `use-pages.ts`) is a list of options that takes the keyboard: the arrow keys choose, with Shift several, with Ctrl or ⌘ they only move on and Space adds the page they stand on; Alt and an arrow move the chosen pages a place on; Delete takes them out; Ctrl or ⌘ Z undoes from there; Escape lets go of the choice, then closes the list. With a mouse, pages are dragged to another place, and the list scrolls to meet the pointer; with a finger the list scrolls, and pages are moved by the buttons or by typing the place. Only the rows near what is seen are drawn. It shares its place with the list of comments: one at a time.
- **Tried on real files** (the nine of the compressor's benchmark, each with a page taken out, the last page moved to the front, a copy, a turn and a blank page): every result opens in pdf.js with no reference that points at nothing and passes `qpdf --check`; pages that only moved compare the same as the originals; a change takes a few milliseconds, the longest 48 ms for taking a page out of a 142-page guide with 45 links and three bookmarks that led to it.
- **Tests:** `pages.test.ts` (100%), `pages.node.test.ts` with the real engine (order, links, bookmarks, fields, tags, attachments and layers after moves; a page taken out with all that led to it, and nothing of it left in any stream; tags with what a figure shows; copies with words of their own; blank pages; turned pages with what was added to them; the file's page numbers; undo and redo; steps before and after a change of pages; a draft; forty changes in a seeded order against the plan), `e2e/pdf-pages.spec.ts` on five profiles (buttons, the keyboard alone, dragging, typing a place, things added to a page that moves and turns, accessibility in both themes and at 320 pixels).

## Pages of another file, and pages as a file (phase 6, second part)

The list of pages has two more buttons: "Pages from a PDF" adds every page of another PDF after the last chosen page (or at the end), and "Save as a file" writes the chosen pages as a file of their own.

- **Another file is made ready once** (`prepareInsert` in `mupdf-pages.ts`), when it is handed over: the merging engine writes it anew as a file of its own, which is what it does for the PDF merger, with everything that has been learnt there. Links by name are given their places; a form's fields are drawn into their pages, since a field would need a place in this file's form, where its name may be taken; a signature becomes its picture; bookmarks are left behind. A locked file wants its password once (a small form in the list asks for it, and says when it is not the one); what comes out is not locked.
- **The step that adds the pages names the file by a name** (`{ do: "insert", at, file, count }`), and the session keeps the file as it was made ready (`session.inserts`). A step has to be made again whenever something is undone, and making a step again cannot wait: so what takes time is done before, and the step itself only opens the ready file, makes each of its pages stand alone and copies them in as whole objects (`graftObject` through one map, so a link from one of its pages to another leads to the copy). Its pages join the run of page numbers they are put into.
- **A draft keeps the file as it was made ready,** beside the pictures a draft keeps (`picturesOf`; the name, `pdf-…`, tells it from a picture, `isInsert`), and hands it back before the steps are made again. So a draft needs no password for a file that was locked, and never holds one.
- **Some pages as a file of their own** (`exportPart`): the whole file is written and checked as for a download, and the pages are then taken out of that by the splitting engine, with the links, bookmarks and fields that still have somewhere to point, and with the lock that is set for downloads. The file in the editor is as it was: what is not yet downloaded stays so. The file is named after its pages (`partName`: `plan-pages-2-to-4.pdf`), and the list says what was saved, and what the check found amiss if anything.
- **Tests:** in `pages.node.test.ts` with the real engine: another file's pages with their links between them, this file's links, bookmarks, form, tags and page numbers after them; a locked file with no, a wrong and the right password; a file that was not handed over, or is not the one that was; a draft that names the file and is taken up with and without it; a part with what still has somewhere to lead, in an order that is asked for, locked. In `e2e/pdf-pages.spec.ts` and `e2e/pdf-draft.spec.ts` on five profiles: a file's pages added, a locked file with a wrong and the right password, what is no PDF, chosen pages saved and read back, and a draft with moved pages and a locked file's pages taken up after the page was closed.

## Crop and page size (phase 6, third part)

"Crop and page size" (Y) lays a frame over the page being read. Its edges are dragged inwards, moved with the arrow keys (ten points at a time with Shift), or typed as numbers in the bar; Enter on an edge, or "Crop", cuts the page to the frame. The cut is for this page, for the pages chosen in the list of pages, or for all pages. "Show whole" shows the whole sheet again, and "Resize" makes pages the size of an A4 or a Letter sheet.

- **Cropping hides; it does not remove.** A PDF page has a sheet and, on it, the part that is shown; cropping makes that part smaller (`reshape` in `mupdf-pages.ts`). What is outside is still in the file, and any program can show it again. The bar says so while the tool is in hand, and the download says so. Taking content out for good is redaction (phase 7).
- **What is cut off is counted from the page as it is shown,** so much off its left, top, right and bottom (`{ do: "crop", pages, off }`), so that one cut fits pages of different sizes. A page the file shows turned stores its sheet unturned: which stored side each shown edge is, is worked out without the engine (`storedEdges`, and back with `shownEdges`), and the box is written by hand in the file's own points. A test cuts pages that are turned by 90, 180 and 270 degrees and reads the box with pdf-lib, and has the engine say where a line of the page lies afterwards: 50 and 20 points nearer the corner.
- **Too little is never left:** a cut that would leave less than 18 points of a page's width or height is refused for all the pages it is for, with the page it fails on; the frame itself cannot be made that small.
- **Another size** (`{ do: "size", pages, size }`) is the merging engine's own fitting of a page to a sheet (`fitPage`, exported for this): the page's content is put inside a matrix, as much smaller or larger as fits the sheet and in its middle; links, notes and fields move and scale with it; a page that lies on its side gets a sheet on its side. A page that is that size already is left alone, and a change that changes nothing is no step.
- **What the editor added stays where it is on what the page shows** through all three: moved by what was cut off (`moved`), or made as much smaller or larger as the page, letters and line weights too, with a measuring line still saying the length it said (`onResizedPage` in `objects.ts`). In the file's own points a thing on a cropped page has not moved, which a test reads with pdf.js.
- **Such a page is not what it was:** in the plan it is `{ was, other: true }`, so a download does not compare it with the original, and the screen is told its new size like any change to the list of pages.
- **The frame** (`crop-layer.tsx`, `use-crop.ts`) is four edges that are buttons; the keyboard is put on the left one without scrolling the pages (an edge is as long as the page, and a browser would scroll to show all of it, which threw the page out of sight on a phone). A press on another page puts the frame there. Any other tool puts this one down.
- **Tests:** `pages.test.ts` and `objects.test.ts` (100%), `pages.node.test.ts` with the real engine (a cut with a thing and a link on the page, cut again and shown whole, undone; turned pages; too large a cut; another size with a thing, a link and a page on its side), `e2e/pdf-crop.spec.ts` on five profiles (the keyboard and typed numbers, a dragged edge, all pages, chosen pages, shown whole, another size, accessibility in both themes and at 320 pixels).

## Numbers, headers and watermarks (phase 6, fourth part)

"Numbers and watermarks" in the bar (Z) opens a panel beside the pages for what is put on many pages at once: page numbers (1, Page 1, 1 of 12, Page 1 of 12, from any first number), a header or footer of one's own (with `{page}`, `{pages}`, `{date}` and `{name}` standing for the page's number, the count, today's date and the file's name), Bates numbers (a number that runs on, with a fixed count of figures and words before and after it), and a watermark of words or of a picture (from corner to corner or level, as solid and as large as is set). Each is for all pages, or for the pages typed into "Pages" (`1-3, 7, 10-`, `odd`, `even`; `parseRanges` of the merger), numbered in the order typed.

- **Each becomes an ordinary thing on each page** (`lib/pdf-editor/repeated.ts`, pure): a line is a text box as wide as the page between its margins, set to the side its place is on; a watermark is a text box or a picture in the middle of the page, turned along the line from corner to corner. They are added in one step, one thing to a page (`repeatedChange`, `editor.repeat`), so one undo takes them all away, and afterwards any one of them is selected, moved, restyled or deleted like anything else that was added. Nothing new was needed in the engine: their fonts are embedded and cut to the letters used, they are read back on download, kept in a draft, and drawn into the page unless "Keep editable" is on, as every added thing is.
- **A step may hold a change for every page:** the most changes in one step went from 2,000 to 20,000 (`MOST_OPS`), the most pages a file may have here.
- **A watermark's words surely fit their box:** their size is worked out from the length the watermark takes and how wide a letter is at most on average, a little more than it is, so the words are never broken into two lines. A picture goes into the file once, however many pages show it.
- **What it says of itself:** numbers are written as the pages lie when they are added, so pages are put in order first; a watermark is laid over the page and does not protect the file. Both are said in the panel, in the help and in the articles.
- **Three doors** open the editor for these jobs: Crop PDF (`/dashboard/crop-pdf/`, the frame is on the page from the start), Add page numbers (`/dashboard/add-page-numbers/`) and Watermark PDF (`/dashboard/watermark-pdf/`), both with the panel open and set for the job. Their bar is narrowed to the pages' own tools (`focus: "pages"`), with the rest behind "All tools".
- **Tests:** `repeated.test.ts` (100%), `e2e/pdf-repeat.spec.ts` on five profiles (numbers on every page read back by pdf.js with where they lie; a header from the second page on; Bates numbers; a page the file does not have; a watermark of words on odd pages, undone in one step; a watermark of a picture; accessibility in both themes and at 320 pixels), `e2e/pdf-page-doors.spec.ts` (each door's title, words, help, guide and place in the list; what each opens with).

## The file as a whole (phase 6, fifth part)

"Details and bookmarks" in the bar opens a panel for the file as a whole, in three parts.

- **Details:** the title, author, subject and keywords the file says of itself, typed and saved as one step. A field that is emptied takes that detail out of the file. A file may say the same things a second time in a packet of its own (XMP), and programs differ in which they believe: that packet is taken out with the change, so that the two cannot disagree and what was changed does not stay there. The download says so.
- **Bookmarks:** click a name to choose a bookmark and to rename it (Enter saves, Escape leaves the name as it was). The buttons above the list add a bookmark for the page being read, move the chosen one up and down, put it beneath the one above or take it out from beneath, lead it to the page being read, and delete it with what is beneath it. The number beside a name is the page it leads to, and goes there. Every change writes the whole list anew (`{ do: "bookmarks", items }`): the list is small, and a list written whole cannot be left half changed. A bookmark keeps the view it had of its page (`retarget`); its colour and weight, which few files set, are not kept.
- **Page numbers** of the file's own (not written on the pages): runs such as i, ii, iii and then 1, 2, 3, each with where it begins, how it is written, what stands before the number and its first number. The panel shows what the first pages will be called before anything is saved.
- **How it is built:** the list with a bookmark added, renamed, moved or taken out, and how a run writes a number, are worked out without the engine (`lib/pdf-editor/document.ts`, pure); `mupdf-document.ts` writes them, with the merger's own writer for bookmarks. They are a kind of step of their own (`document`) that touches no page, so every page is still compared with the original on download. The page is told what the file says of itself whenever such a step is made, undone or made again, and whenever pages are put otherwise, since bookmarks then lead to other places (`described` on the session, `document` in `EditNews`).
- **Tests:** `document.test.ts` (100%), `document.node.test.ts` with the real engine (details read, changed, emptied, undone; a second description taken out; bookmarks renamed, led elsewhere, added, put beneath another and deleted, read back by pdf.js, following pages that move; all taken away, and made in a file that had none; runs of page numbers; a draft), `e2e/pdf-document.spec.ts` on five profiles.

## One editor, several doors

There is one PDF editor. The annotator, Sign PDF, Fill PDF form, Crop PDF, Add page numbers and Watermark PDF are **doors**: each is a catalogue entry with `partOf: "pdf-editor"`. A door has an address, a title, a description, written help and an article of its own, is in the sitemap and the offline copy, and is found by the search in the header. It is not a card on the tools page, not counted in the sidebar and not an app shortcut (`listed` in `lib/catalogue.ts` is the tools without their doors), and the article that goes through every tool names it under the editor. Each page says what it is and links to the other.

A door must be **focused**: it shows the tools of its job and puts the others one press away. A door that offered everything the editor does was a second copy of the editor in the list, which is what this replaced. The doors planned for later phases (Redact PDF and the rest) are made the same way.

## How it is built

### The document is the truth

The worker holds a **working copy** of the file. It is opened as it is (`openPdf(..., untouched)`), not through the merger's `prepare`, which rewrites pages. Every change the page asks for is made in that document, and the page is drawn again from it. There is no second model of the document on the page that could drift from the file.

- **One change is one step** (`inStep`): it is made inside one operation of MuPDF's journal (`beginOperation`, `endOperation`), so a change that fails is taken back whole, and it is listed in `EditSession.steps` as what was asked for (`Done`), what it is called and the pages it changed.
- **Undo makes the working copy anew.** `stepBack` opens the file as it came and makes the steps still in force again, in order (`madeAgain`); redo makes the next step again on the working copy as it is. The engine's own `undo()` is never called. The engine remembers where in a file it has put each font and picture, and its undo takes them out without its forgetting: the next text in that font, or the same picture placed again, pointed at an object that was gone, the engine read its own result without noticing, and other programs showed the text without its font. A fresh copy remembers nothing. An undo after 300 steps takes about 70 ms; the cost grows with the number of steps, not with the size of the file. A step that fails is followed by the same remaking, for the same reason.
- **Each module that makes changes says how its kind is made again** (`canDo`), so a step can be made again whichever module made it first. The worker is told when the working copy has become another document (`moved`), since the reader draws from the same one.
- **Things that are added are Stamp annotations while editing.** Each has an appearance the editor writes itself (a display list: text by glyph in our fonts, paths, the picture), and carries what it is as JSON under `/KeepSimpObject`. That key is how the editor finds its own things again; a note from elsewhere that happens to carry something under the key is not taken for one (`readObject` checks every field).
- **The appearance is set last,** with `setAppearanceFromDisplayList`, and `update()` is not called after it. Otherwise MuPDF writes an appearance of its own over it or reshapes the rectangle.
- **A page object is destroyed and loaded again after every change and every undo** (`withPage`). A page that is kept across a change draws what it held before.
- **Pages the file shows turned** (a `/Rotate` of 90, 180 or 270) are edited as they are shown: points are counted from the top left of the page as the reader shows it, and the appearance is turned back with the page's own turn.

### Downloading

`exportEdited` never tidies the working copy: saving with `garbage` renumbers objects, and the comments and fields that later steps name by number would no longer be found. It writes the working copy out as it is, reads that in again, and does everything to the second copy:

1. **Flatten** (unless "Keep editable"): each of our annotations' appearances is drawn into its page's content, and the annotation is removed. MuPDF's `bake` is all or nothing, and would take the file's own notes and form fields with it.
2. **Cut the fonts.** Our fonts go in whole (about 400 KB each) and are cut down to the letters used with `subsetFonts`. The engine calls that step experimental, so the cut is kept only if every page with added text, and a spread of the others, reads and draws the same before and after. Both sides of that comparison are read from the same written file: a picture that was just placed is drawn from what it was made of, and a hair differently once it has been written, which must not be taken for a fault of the cut. A file with form fields is not cut.
3. **The check.** The words of everything that has letters (text boxes, call-outs, stamps, what measuring lines say) are looked for in the result's text (word by word, since boxes that overlap are read mixed together), and pages nothing was added to are drawn from the original and from the result and compared pixel for pixel (`pagesToCheck` chooses up to twelve, spread through the file).
4. **Write,** through `shareDuplicates` and `garbage: 2` like the other tools, with the lock when one is set.

The report (`ExportReport`) becomes the lines in the download card (`lib/pdf-editor/report.ts`). The first line always says what became of what was added (part of the page, or kept movable), so that a download never comes without a word: a one-page file with a single shape has nothing else to check. A line that is not good news is red and says which pages to look at.

### The page

- `components/pdf-editor/use-editor.ts` is the state for one open file. Every change goes to the worker (`edit-apply`) and comes back as news: the pages that changed, with what they now hold, and the list of changes.
- **A change is shown at once.** What the pages hold is "what the file last said" plus the changes asked for since (`applied` in `lib/pdf-editor/tools.ts`), so the frame and hit area of a thing move without waiting, and the next change is worked out from where things are on the screen. The file's answer replaces the guess; a change that fails is taken back and the bar says why.
- **A press with the text tool keeps the keyboard in the new box.** The layer cancels the browser's own mouse-down while the text tool is in hand or a box is being typed: on any press the browser moves the keyboard to the pages, which closed a box that had just been opened, once the font was loaded and the box opened at once. A journey types three boxes in a row for that reason.
- **Arrow keys are gathered:** moves that follow each other within 400 ms go to the file as one change, so a held key is one step of undo. Anything else that is asked for sends what was gathered first.
- `components/pdf-editor/page-layer.tsx` is laid over each page through the reader's `overlay` slot, in the page's own points. It holds a button for every thing, the frame and handles of the selection, guides, what a gesture is about to make, and the box being typed in.
- **Typing** happens in a `<textarea>` in the same font file that is put into the PDF, with kerning and ligatures off, since the file is written letter by letter, each as wide as the font says. Lines are broken by the page (`breakLines`, measured with a canvas) and sent with the box, so the file has the lines that were on the screen. Phones zoom the whole page in on a field with letters under 16 pixels, so the field is made larger and scaled back down.
- **Typing ends once.** Leaving a box can be noticed twice (by the box losing the keyboard and by what was pressed), and Safari does not give a pressed button the keyboard at all; `finishTyping` clears a ref before anything else. A press on the bar does not take the keyboard from the box, so a look can be chosen while typing.
- **New words stay until the page shows them.** When a line or paragraph of the file has been changed, the page is drawn again from the file, and that takes a moment. The field went away at once, so the old words were seen again for that moment and then jumped to the new ones. The reader now says which version of a page its picture shows (`PageDrawn` in `components/pdf-reader/reader-page.tsx`, and `data-drawn` on the page), and the words that were typed are kept over the line (`SettlingWords` in `retext-layer.tsx`, hidden from screen readers) until the picture is of a later version than the one they were typed over. A change that is refused takes them away at once.
- **A number is taken when the typing of it pauses.** The size of text, the size of a line and the count of boxes are typed into a `NumberField` (`toolbar.tsx`): it keeps what is typed, also what is no number yet, and sends it after half a second without a key, when the field is left, on Enter, or on a press anywhere else (before that press does its own work, so a box started with it has the size). Sent at every key, "24" was first "2", which is smaller than text may be, and the field snapped back under the hand. The see-through slider (`Slide`) is the same: a drag across it is one change. A number beyond the bounds is brought to the nearest that is allowed once typing is over, never while it goes on.
- **The pages stay where they are while a field has the keyboard.** End, Home, Page Up and Page Down move nothing in a field whose caret is at the end already, and on a Mac they are keys for scrolling wherever the keyboard is: the browser then scrolled the pages to their end, and whoever was typing in a line was thrown off the page, the line's field with it. The reader sees such a key before the field does (`keepPages` in `components/pdf-reader/pdf-reader.tsx`), moves the caret itself (to the start or the end of its line; of everything for the Page keys and with Ctrl or ⌘; with Shift what lies between is selected) and tells the browser that the key is dealt with. Holding the pages still and undoing the scroll, which was tried first, did not always hold on Linux.
- **The keyboard is brought back only to where it was taken from.** After a change, frames are made anew and the window takes the keyboard for itself, so a line that was typed in, and the page of the list the keyboard stood on, take it back over the next two frames. If the keyboard has been taken somewhere else in that moment (to another line, or to a field of the list), it stays there: a line that is given the keyboard lets go of the hold (`onFocus` in `retext-layer.tsx`), and so does the list when anything but a page in it is given the keyboard. Before that, Delete pressed straight after leaving a line took out the line that was left.
- **The tools are a pure state machine** (`press`, `drag`, `release` in `lib/pdf-editor/tools.ts`): from a pointer's place and the things on a page to a selection, a gesture, or a change. The screen only feeds it and draws what it says.

## Rules

- Only `lib/pdf-editor/mupdf-edit.ts`, `mupdf-comments.ts`, `mupdf-forms.ts`, `mupdf-signed.ts` and `mupdf-text.ts` touch the engine, and each is handed the engine, so the same code runs in the worker and in Node.
- Nothing a file brings with it is run. A form's script is read as text at most (to learn how a date is written).
- Never save the working copy with `garbage`, and never keep a page object across a change.
- Never call the engine's `undo()` or `redo()`, and never save the working copy with `incremental`. A change is undone by making the working copy anew, and written after a signed file from a fresh copy.
- A result is checked for references that point at nothing (`danglingRefs` in `tests/pdf-reader.ts`) wherever a test undoes something. The engine does not notice them in its own files.
- Everything that comes from a file or from the page goes through `readObject` before it is drawn. A thing it does not know is left alone.
- An appearance is set last, with no `update()` after it.
- What the editor says a tool does is what it does. "Cover" must never be described as removing anything.
- A check that compares two versions of a file reads both from written bytes.
- Never use the engine's `rearrangePages`: it drops tags, attachments, page numbers and the form's list of fields. Never ask anything of an object that may not be there (`get` on what `get` returned for a missing key fails inside the engine): look whether it is a dictionary first.

## Limits

- Form fields cannot be added yet (phase 8).
- Page numbers and headers are written once, as the pages lie then: they do not follow pages that are moved afterwards. A watermark lies over the page, not under it, and a colour or picture behind a page's content cannot be set yet. What is put on many pages is afterwards a thing on each page, with no way yet to change all of them together except undo.
- Cropping hides what is outside the frame and leaves it in the file. A page is made the size of an A4 or a Letter sheet, not of a size typed in. Pages added from another file come as they look: a form's fields on them are part of the page, that file's bookmarks and tags are not brought along, and a layer it shows hidden is shown. All of a file's pages are added; the ones that are not wanted are taken out afterwards. A copy of a page does not have the form fields of its page, and is not part of the file's tags. Bookmarks cannot be added or renamed yet. A bookmark that led to a page that was taken out, and has others beneath it, stays and leads nowhere.
- A picture of the page that is cut to a part is whole in the file: the rest is hidden, not removed. Pictures inside something else the page draws, and pictures written into the page's content itself, are not offered. A picture is turned by quarters only. Its own data is never changed: it cannot be made lighter or sharper here.
- A change to a line stays within its line: a longer line runs on to the right. A paragraph flows within its width and may grow downwards only into empty room: nothing below it moves. Which lines are a paragraph is judged by their look and can be judged wrongly; "Paragraphs" in the bar turns it off. A compound word that a file broke at its hyphen at the end of a line, and writes nowhere else, loses that hyphen when its paragraph flows again unless it is typed back (the field names such words). Letter spacing a line was set with (kerning, justification) is not kept: the new letters stand where their own widths put them. Text written downwards and text in scripts whose letters join can be taken out but not retyped. A line's font can be changed to one of the editor's or to a font of one's own, not to another font of the file. A line that is moved or made larger until it lies over another line may be refused, since the page could then no longer be read back line by line; on five real documents 58 changes of words, size, colour, font and place were made and 5 refused in this way or because a line could not be taken out without its neighbour, each time with nothing changed.
- A form's scripts are never run: a field a script would format or work out holds what was typed. XFA forms are not read. Read-only fields, push buttons and places for a certificate signature are left as they are.
- A list's look is ours, in Helvetica: a letter that font lacks is left out of the picture of the list (the choice itself is kept). A drop-down shows the value the file keeps, which a few forms make different from what they show.
- A date field takes three presses of Tab in some browsers, one for each part of the date.
- A signature is a mark on the page, not a certificate: it does not prove who signed, and a file that already carries a digital signature no longer counts as signed once it is changed (the editor says so when such a file is opened).
- Taking the paper out of a picture of a signature needs light, plain paper. Lined or dark paper, or a shadow across the photo, is taken for ink.
- A mark on words cannot be moved or stretched once made; it is deleted and made again. A comment of a kind the editor does not make keeps its place and its look.
- A page that is a picture has no words to mark; the highlighter pen is what works there until OCR arrives (phase 9).
- Drawn into the page, a note is only its icon.
- A call-out has one colour for its words, frame and arrow, a white inside, and does not turn. A measuring line measures the page: it knows a drawing's scale only when told.
- A font of one's own is used as it is: no bold or italic is made from it, a font its maker restricts is not embedded, and web fonts (WOFF) are not read. The device's fonts can be listed only where the browser allows it (Chromium on a computer); elsewhere a font file is chosen.
- A font has the letters its file has: Latin, Greek and Cyrillic among them. A letter without a shape is left out of the file, and the bar names it. Text that needs shaping (Arabic, Indic scripts) is not supported.
- Text in a box that is kept editable is a stamp to other apps: they show it, and may let a reader move or delete it.
- The fonts are fetched when text is first typed; each is about 400 KB.
- One file at a time, up to the device's limit for one file (300 MB on a computer, 100 MB on a phone).

- **Signing:** `signature.test.ts` (100%), the signing part of `marks.node.test.ts` with the real engine (a typed signature read by pdf.js in a hand font cut to its letters; a drawn one, a tick, a cross and a dot in the page's pixels), and `e2e/pdf-signer.spec.ts` on five profiles: drawn, typed and pictured signatures (the picture read back from the download with its paper see-through and its ink still blue), initials, the date, ticks and crosses, remembering and forgetting, things landing where the page is being read, and the window's accessibility in each of its states.
- **Forms:** `forms.test.ts` (100%), `forms.node.test.ts` with the real engine (every kind read with its label, limits and place, also on a turned page; every kind filled in and read back by pdf-lib and pdf.js; round buttons across pages; a field shown on two pages; clearing; undo and redo; what is refused makes no step; a list's tinted bar in the page's pixels, upright on a turned page; fields made part of the page with their words read by pdf.js, in a locked result), and `e2e/pdf-form-filler.spec.ts` on five profiles: every kind by mouse and keyboard, Tab order, Escape, what is missing and the way to it, typing that is still under way at Download, the final copy, files with no form, XFA and scripts, a tool drawn over a field, the sample, axe in every state.
- **After an undo** (`undo.node.test.ts`, and a journey in `e2e/pdf-editor.spec.ts`): text in a font, a picture and a filled field, each made, undone and made again, give results in which no reference points at nothing (`danglingRefs`, read with pdf-lib) and whose words and pictures other software reads. The seeded run of many edits checks the same.
- **Drafts:** `draft.test.ts` (100%), `draft.node.test.ts` with the real engine (steps kept as text and read back make a fresh copy what the session's copy was, undo list and all; a draft whose font was not handed over is not taken up and leaves the file as it came), and `e2e/pdf-draft.spec.ts` on five profiles: nothing kept with the switch off; a field, a text box, a picture and an undone step kept, the page reloaded, the draft taken up, redone and downloaded; thrown away; switched off and on; steps that are not steps offered as nothing.
- **Offline** (`e2e/offline.spec.ts`): text is typed, a signature is typed in a hand font, and both are downloaded with the server stopped, which needs the fonts from the saved copy.

## The phases

1. **The workspace, and adding things.** Built.
2. **Mark up and comment.** Built: marks on words, the highlighter pen, notes and replies, the list of comments, comments a file comes with, the PDF annotator page, stamps, call-outs and measuring lines.
3. **Fill and sign.** Built so far: drawn, typed and pictured signatures, initials, the date, ticks and crosses, and the Sign PDF page. Also built: filling in a form's own fields, with the Fill PDF form page. Also built: saving after a signed file so that its signature stays valid, drafts kept on the device when asked, and text in the rows of boxes of forms without fields. The phase is complete.
4. **Change the text that is there,** in the file's own font where it has the letters, with the old words gone from the file. A line's words are changed or taken out, its size, colour and font changed, and the line moved, also when it is turned; text can be set in a font of one's own, from a file or from the device.
5. **Paragraphs, pictures in the page, links, find and replace.** Lines that belong together are changed as one paragraph that flows again, and links go with their words; the pictures a page already has are moved, resized, turned, cut to a part, exchanged and taken out; links are added, led somewhere else, moved and taken away; words are found through the whole file and replaced, one place or all.
6. **Pages and the document.** Built: pages moved, turned, copied, taken out and blank ones added, from a list beside the pages; the pages of another PDF added, and chosen pages saved as a file of their own; pages cropped, shown whole again and made another size; page numbers, headers and footers, Bates numbers and watermarks; bookmarks added, renamed, moved, nested and deleted; the file's title and other details; the numbers it gives its own pages. Focused pages: Crop PDF, Add page numbers, Watermark PDF. The phase is complete.
7. **Redact and protect:** by area, text and pattern, removed and proved. Focused page: Redact PDF.
8. **Make forms.** Focused page: Create a fillable PDF form.
9. **OCR on the device,** videos, a benchmark beside other editors. Focused page: OCR PDF.
10. **Certificate signatures.**

Not planned: running a file's scripts, XFA forms, turning pages into pictures, uploads, accounts.

### What the plan was based on

- Sejda uploads files and allows three tasks an hour on files of up to 50 MB or 200 pages ([TechRadar](https://techradar.com/reviews/sejda)).
- Firefox's built-in editor adds text, drawings, highlights, comments, images and signatures; it does not change words that are already in the file ([Mozilla](https://www.firefox.com/features/pdf-editor/)).
- "Whiteout" in many tools only covers words; they can still be copied out ([PDF Candy](https://pdfcandy.com/edit-pdf.html)).
- MuPDF 1.28.1, probed during planning: it removes one line of text with `applyRedactions` and leaves the rest, journals changes, and writes text by glyph in an embedded font. It does not sign with certificates, create form fields, run scripts or shape Arabic and Indic text.
