# Video workspace

Updated 23 October 2026. Applies to `/dashboard/video-editor/` and its doors. **Phases 1 to 3 are built:** converting between MP4, WebM, MOV and MKV, many files at once, each result checked before it is offered; making videos smaller, to a size or a quality, with how close they look measured; and trimming, without encoding or to the frame, split into files, with frames saved as pictures. It follows the audio workspace (`docs/audio-workspace.md`): one tool, several doors, every choice worked out in one place and shown as a sentence, every result opened again and checked before it is offered, and results checked with FFmpeg and FFprobe, never with Mediabunny alone.

## Research

Reviewed 22 October 2026. Browser support changes; capability is always probed in the browser at the real size, never assumed from its name.

## What it does (phase 1)

- **One workspace, doors into it.** The Video editor is the tool; the Video converter (`/dashboard/video-converter/`) is a door (`partOf: "video-editor"`), with its own title, help and article. The Video compressor and the Video trimmer are doors too (phases 2 and 3); nothing in the catalogue is still to come.
- **Open** up to 20 videos at once, of any length, by drop or picker, or the sample: MP4, MOV, WebM, MKV and MPEG-TS, told apart by their first bytes (`lib/video/formats.ts`). AVI, FLV, WMV and MPEG-PS are named and refused with what to do; anything else says it is not a video read here.
- **Each file** shows its kind, the picture's codec, size as shown (after its own turn), frame rate (and whether it varies), length and size, a small picture from near its start, and a player for the original and the result.
- **Formats:** MP4 (H.264, HEVC, AV1, VP9), WebM (VP9, AV1, VP8), MOV (H.264, HEVC), MKV (any of them), and "Same as the file" (MPEG-TS becomes MP4). What each holds is held to Mediabunny's own writers by a test.
- **Copy first.** Every choice is worked out in `planFor` (`lib/video/settings.ts`) and shown as sentences under the player. The picture is copied as it is when the kind holds its codec, plays it everywhere (MP4 and MOV copy only H.264 and HEVC unless another codec is chosen on purpose), and nothing about it is to change. It is encoded again for a chosen quality, a smaller size, a lower frame rate, another codec, or a turn the kind cannot carry. Copies may shift every track together by up to a second (`copy.shiftTolerance`), so a file whose sound starts before zero (an AAC encoder's priming) is still copied, in step.
- **Encoding.** The browser's own encoders (WebCodecs), asked first what they can write at the file's size (`canEncodeVideo`, `lib/video/probe.ts`); without a choice, the first the kind holds that can be written (H.264 for MP4, VP9 for WebM). Sizes are always even and set explicitly (Safari refuses odd H.264 sizes). Bitrates: High is YouTube's recommended upload bitrate for the shorter side and frame rate, interpolated by pixels; Balanced half and Small a quarter (KeepSimp's choices); HEVC and VP9 0.6 and AV1 0.4 of H.264's (Mediabunny's estimates); never more than the source holds for its size and codec; Custom from 100 kbit/s to 200 Mbit/s. A keyframe every 2 s. A turned picture is drawn upright (`allowTransformationMetadata: false`) whenever it is encoded.
- **Size and frame rate** cap the shorter side (4K to 360p) and the frames a second (60 to 15); neither is ever raised. **Turn and mirror:** in MP4 and MOV a turn of a copied picture is a setting players follow; elsewhere the picture is drawn turned.
- **Sound:** copied when the kind plays it everywhere (AAC and MP3 in MP4; PCM too in MOV; Opus and Vorbis in WebM; almost anything in MKV), converted otherwise or when asked (AAC in MP4 and MOV, Opus in WebM and MKV), never at more than the file's own bitrate, or left out. AAC is written by the browser's encoder when its trial keeps every sample, or by FFmpeg's (the audio workspace's `aacEncoding`).
- **In step.** An encoder puts a run of samples before the sound (1,024 for FFmpeg's AAC, 2,112 for Apple's), which plays as a delay: measured 21.5 ms and 44.2 ms before this was handled. In MP4 and MOV an edit list on the sound track skips the measured priming (`lib/video/sound-start.ts`), written while the index is moved; WebM and MKV get Opus, whose header carries its own skip. AAC in MKV is used only where a browser writes neither Opus nor Vorbis, and the page then says it starts a few hundredths of a second late.
- **Writing to disk.** Results stream into the page's own folder in the origin private file system (`lib/video/scratch.ts`, a sync access handle in the worker), so a video of any length is never held in memory; where that storage is missing (some private windows) the result is held in memory. Each page's folder is emptied when it goes; a folder left by a closed page is emptied after six hours, the next time the workspace opens. The privacy page says so.
- **Fast start.** MP4 and MOV are written with their index last, then the index is moved in front (`lib/video/faststart.ts`): only the index is read and changed; the result is the written file's slices in another order, so nothing large is copied. Fragmented files, indexes over 256 MB and offsets that would pass 4 GB in a 32-bit table are left as they are, and the page says so.
- **The check** (`lib/video/check.ts`): every result is opened again before it is offered: its kind, the picture's codec and size as shown, its frame rate when one was set, the sound's presence and codec, its length within 0.15 s or two and a half frames of the original's, and its first and last frames and its sound decoded. A result that does not hold is shown with what is wrong; agents are not handed one.
- **Download** one video, or all as a ZIP made of the files themselves (`lib/video/zip-blobs.ts`: stored, CRCs read a piece at a time, ZIP64 beyond 4 GB).
- **Stopping.** A worker writing a file that new settings outdate retires and is replaced (as in the audio workspace), so a stopped encode never holds up the next.
- **Not kept:** a file's own subtitles and chapters; HDR is written without tone mapping (the page says so).

## The compressor (phase 2)

- **The door:** the Video compressor (`/dashboard/video-compressor/`, `partOf: "video-editor"`) opens with the size limit first and set to half of each file, and its own sample (`public/samples/keepsimp-scene.mp4`: six seconds of a zooming Mandelbrot set at 960 × 540, made by `scripts/sample-video.sh`, 1.6 MB so it stays in the offline copy).
- **A size limit** (`limit` in the settings, worked out in `planFor` with `lib/video/target.ts`): 8, 10, 25, 50 or 100 MB, half or a quarter of each file, or one's own from 0.25 MB (1 MB is 1,048,576 bytes, as elsewhere here). The sound is planned first: copied when it takes at most a fifth of the limit's bits, otherwise converted to a tenth of them (AAC at 48 kbit/s at least, Opus 32). The picture gets the rest, less 1.5% and 16 KB for the container. When that leaves fewer than 0.05 bits for each pixel of each frame in H.264 (HandBrake's "Social" presets come to about 0.05 at every step: 25 MB for 30 s of 1080p60, a minute of 720p60, two of 540p60, five of 360p60), more than 30 frames a second become 30, then the shorter side steps down (2160, 1440, 1080, 720, 540, 480, 360, 240); HEVC and VP9 need 0.6 of that and AV1 0.4. Each codec is fitted and then asked of the browser at its own size. A limit too small even at 240p is refused with the size the video can come to. A file already under its limit is copied.
- **Passes** (the worker's `writePass`): browser encoders have no second pass and do not always keep to a bitrate, so the bytes written are watched (`target.on("write")`) and, once a quarter is done, a pass that will end over a tenth above the limit is stopped and begun again with the bitrate scaled by limit over forecast, less 3%; a pass that ends over the limit, or under 85% of it, is written again the same way; three passes at most. An encoder can have a floor: Firefox's H.264 on Linux (found in CI, then measured in the Playwright image) writes about 1.8 Mbit/s for the 960 × 540 sample whatever it is asked below that, and Chromium's has a lower one. So a pass that writes over half again what its bitrate asked (`FLOOR_OVERSHOOT`) is followed by one at the same bitrate and the largest size step with fewer pixels by as much as it wrote over (`nextPass`, `stepDown`); the result says the size it was made (`floorSaid`). The last pass under the limit is kept; a result over it is shown with what is wrong and never handed to an agent. The page says which pass is running, and how many it took.
- **How close it looks** (`lib/video/looks.ts`, `looksAlike`): for a picture encoded again and not turned, six frames are drawn at the same moments (from a tenth to nine tenths of the way) from the original and the result, at most 480 px wide, and compared with the image compressor's SSIM on luma. The page gives the mean and the lowest frame, in numbers and in words (KeepSimp's own: 0.99 hard to tell apart, 0.95 very close, 0.90 close, under that noticeably softer).

## The trimmer (phase 3, part 1)

- **The door:** the Video trimmer (`/dashboard/video-trimmer/`, `partOf: "video-editor"`) opens with the trim panel shown and the format kept as each file's own (`format: "same"`), with the compressor's sample, which has a keyframe every two seconds. The editor shows the panel too; the converter and compressor have it one press away (Trim). Results of a trim are named `-trimmed` when they keep their kind.
- **What is kept** (`lib/video/trim.ts`): parts in the source's own seconds, put in order and joined where they meet (`tidy`); Keep the selection keeps only that stretch of what is kept now (`keepOnly`), Cut it out takes it away (`cutOut`); each edit is one step of the file's `Trim`, undone and done again as one (`trimmed`, `undone` in `lib/video/queue.ts`). The trim is taken up like the settings, once its edits have settled for 600 ms, and the file being written for the trim before it is stopped (`forget`). The selection is set by typing a time (`parseClock`: `7.25`, `1:07.25`, `1:02:03`), dragging, or I and O at the playhead.
- **The panel** (`components/video/trim-panel.tsx`): a timeline of the parts kept, the selection, the playhead and the keyframes (up to 400; read with the facts by `keyframesOf`, at most 50,000), a slider each for the playhead and the selection's ends, the parts kept listed as links to their starts, Play what is kept (steps over what is cut, on a timer), and Save this frame. The player moves by the file's own frame. The keys are listed once (`lib/video/shortcuts.ts`): the help is made from `shortcutGroups`, and they work while the panel has the focus, leaving a field, a slider, a button and a choice their own keys.
- **Lossless** (copied) unless asked: the picture can begin only at a keyframe, so a single part kept in an MP4 or MOV starts exactly where asked by an edit list (the earlier frames kept only to be decoded) and ends at the next keyframe; WebM and MKV parts and several parts are moved out to the keyframes around them, and the plan says how much more that keeps (`snapOut`, `snapSaid`). A copy always ends at a keyframe or the file's end: a part copied to a point between keyframes would leave out frames the last pictures depend on (B-frames), found in the Node test. Several parts are joined by copying packets in decode order, each part from its keyframe, the sound cut to the same times (`joinParts`).
- **Exact** cuts to the frame and the sound to the sample by encoding the picture again with the quality chosen (Original keeps close to its bitrate): Mediabunny's `trim` for one part; for several, `process` hooks drop the frames between parts and fade the sound over 4 ms (`JOIN_FADE`) at each join (`lib/video/cut.ts`). A cut becomes exact by itself when the picture is encoded anyway, when a file has no keyframes listed, or when several parts are joined and the sound must be converted.
- **Splitting** (part 2): points are marked at the playhead (S) or every so many seconds (`everyPoints`), drawn on the timeline, and Make files turns the file into a file for each piece that keeps something (`splitParts`, `splitItem` in `lib/video/queue.ts`), in its place in the list, named `-part-1`, `-part-2` (`partName`). Like the audio workspace's, a part is the file with one more keep step (its own `Trim`), never a second way of cutting: it is planned, written and checked as any trim. The list's limit of 20 counts the parts.
- **Pictures along the timeline:** ten frames read from the file on the page once the trimmer is open (`stripOf`, Mediabunny's `CanvasSink` at 48 px high), never from the player.
- **A frame** is saved as a PNG read from the file by `frameAt` (Mediabunny's `CanvasSink`, the turn applied) on the page, never taken from the player.
- **The check** holds the result's length to the parts kept (`plan.length`), within the usual tolerance.

## Agents (phase 1)

`video_info` and `convert_video` (`lib/agent/video.ts`) read and write through the workspace's own worker (the agent page's own pool of one) and plan with `browserPlan` (`lib/video/jobs.ts`), which asks the browser as `planFor` needs, first choice first. `convert_video` takes every setting the page has and hands over only a result the check found as asked. `trim_video` (phase 3) takes the parts to keep and to cut in seconds of the original (`kept`), lossless or `exact`, with the other settings, and keeps each file's own format unless one is given; `video_frame` saves the frame at a moment as a PNG through `frameAt`, handed over only when its header gives the picture's size. `split_video` splits at points or every so many seconds, after `keep` and `cut`, writing each piece as `trim_video` writes a trim (`splitParts`, `partName`).

## Verification (phases 1 to 3)

- **Unit, 100%:** `formats`, `settings`, `faststart`, `sound-start`, `check`, `queue`, `jobs`, `zip-blobs`, `target`, `looks`, `trim`, `cut`, `shortcuts` and the agent tools. The codec lists are held to Mediabunny's writers.
- **Against FFmpeg in Node** (`faststart.node.test.ts`, `video.node.test.ts`, `zip-blobs.node.test.ts`): fast start on FFmpeg's MP4 and three-track MOV with every frame's hash unchanged; facts read as FFprobe reads them (H.264 and AAC, VP9 and Opus, HEVC in MKV, a phone's turn); copies MP4 → MKV → MP4 with every packet unchanged, a turn read by FFprobe, sound left out, MOV, and MPEG-TS to MP4 with every frame unchanged; ZIPs read by Info-ZIP's `unzip` and Python's `zipfile`, ZIP64 included. Cutting (`cut.node.test.ts`, a file with a keyframe every second and B-frames): one part copied into MP4 is an unbroken run of the source's decoded frames from within a frame of the start asked to the keyframe after the end; MKV parts are exactly the source's frames between their keyframes; two parts joined into MP4 and MKV are exactly the source's frames of both, with the sound within 50 ms of the picture's length and FFmpeg decoding them without a complaint.
- **Browsers** (`e2e/video-workspace.spec.ts`, `e2e/agent-video.spec.ts`, five profiles, results read by FFprobe and FFmpeg): the sample copied with every packet unchanged and its index in front; WebM with VP9 and Opus, all 90 frames, decoding cleanly; a 720p clip made 360p at 15 fps (45 frames) and smaller; a turn by setting and by redrawing (180 × 320), sound left out; AVI and text refused in words; a ZIP of two; the sound within 4 ms of the picture in MP4, WebM and MKV (a flash and a beep at 1 s); axe in both themes and 320 px. The trimmer (`e2e/video-trimmer.spec.ts`): a part kept without encoding is an unbroken run of the source's frames from the frame asked (FFmpeg's frame hashes), its sound 3.5 s; a part cut out exactly with the keys, undone and done again, is 150 frames and 5 s of sound, decoding cleanly; a saved frame is a 640 × 360 PNG; a file split at its keyframes with S is three files of exactly the source's frames between the marks, and the timeline shows ten pictures; the converter has the trimmer one press away; and for agents, a trim and a frame of the sample, and the scene sample split at its keyframes into three files of exactly its frames. Where a browser cannot write a codec, the tests check that the page says so.

## The plan

Each phase is tested (unit 100% on its pure modules, the browser on five profiles, results read by FFprobe and FFmpeg), documented, pushed to `main` and checked live before the next.

1. **Foundation and the converter.** Built. Mediabunny 1.62.0 (the audio checks rerun). `lib/video/`: file kinds by their bytes, the facts (container, codecs, size, rotation, frame rate and whether it varies, length, bitrate, HDR, tracks), capability probes, `planFor` (container, codec, even size, frame rate, quality, copy or encode, the sound's codec) shown as a sentence, a worker that streams each conversion and is replaced when a job is stopped, a check that opens every result again (tracks, length, frame count, first and last frame decoded), and a queue of up to 20 files with ZIP download. Outputs: MP4 (H.264, HEVC, AV1), WebM (VP9, AV1, VP8), MOV, MKV; "same as the file" copies where it can. Size, frame rate, rotation, flips, sound kept, converted, taken out, or only the sound. The Video editor joins the catalogue; the Video converter becomes its door. Article, social card, agent tools `video_info` and `convert_video`.
2. **The compressor.** Built. Quality presets with sourced bitrates, a target size or a percentage, the size ladder, a live forecast while writing, early stop and up to three passes, and what was done said in words; a quality reading (SSIM on frames spread through the clip, checked against FFmpeg's `ssim`). Destination presets with their sources and dates. Door, article, agent tool `compress_video`.
3. **The trimmer.** Built: keeping and cutting parts, lossless and exact, the keys, saving a frame, splitting into files, pictures of frames along the timeline, the door, the article and the agent tools `trim_video`, `split_video` and `video_frame`. As planned: a timeline of frames drawn from the file, the playhead, frame stepping, keyframes marked, the keys listed once (`lib/video/shortcuts.ts`), start and end typed or set with I and O, several parts kept or cut, split into parts, a frame saved as a picture. Lossless (copy) by default and exact on request, with the page saying which and why. Door, article, agent tools `trim_video`, `split_video`, `video_frame`.
4. **Picture and sound.** Crop with shape presets, speed, fades, mute or replace the sound, the sound's loudness through the audio workspace's own engine, text and a logo on every frame (the animation converter's drawing), and turning a clip into a GIF through the animation converter. Agent tools grow to match.
5. **Joining and subtitles.** Clips joined in order (copied when their codecs match, encoded otherwise), and subtitles (SRT or VTT) written on the picture. Agent tools `join_videos` and the subtitle option.

## Not offered

- Keeping a file's own subtitles or chapters: Mediabunny cannot read them yet. The page says they are left out.
- HDR to SDR tone mapping. An HDR file is converted as it is and the page says so.
- A WebAssembly encoder for browsers without WebCodecs (above).
